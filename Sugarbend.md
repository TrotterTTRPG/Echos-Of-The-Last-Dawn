# Sugarbend

Custom content: [[Rules/Custom Items and Spells/Custom Items and Spells|Custom Items and Spells]].

A working fairy community downstream of [[Sessions/Potential Events/Electric Woman|the old woman's generator]], primarily occupied with collecting and extracting sugar from the water. The village supplies both local consumption and an established sugar trade with the Umbral.

## Layout and approach

The pond is **kidney-shaped**. The party’s arrival point and nectar banquet are on the outskirts beside one bank, with only scattered mushroom homes visible. The inward bend of the shoreline hides the main settlement. Oren leads the party along the bank and around that bend, revealing the rest of Sugarbend beside the farther curve of the pond. The village reveal and his account of its history are prepared for the next scene, not already played.

## Established direction

The fairies generally belong in the Umbral. They are not stranded on the material plane by closed crossings or an inability to return. Their dependence on sugar is what keeps them tied to this place.

Sugar is both something the local fairies consume and something they carry back to the Umbral to sell. A large number of fairies there are now addicted too. Sugarbend's interest in the supply therefore extends beyond the needs of its own inhabitants.

The principal motivations are:

- **Protecting established trade.** Keeping sugar flowing preserves an enterprise the fairies have already built.
- **Keeping dependent fairies supplied during an existing crisis.** Reserves are depleted and sugar is scarce. They are already barely staving off problems among fairies who have not had their fix, with a large addicted population in the Umbral adding pressure beyond Sugarbend itself.

Specific Umbral buyers, intermediaries, or powerful interests may remain loosely defined. No particular patron, debt, advance payment, shipment deadline, or compulsory-service arrangement has been established.

## People

**Oren Reedwick** is the elder; **Vetch Bitterleaf** is the doctor/herbalist; **[[NPCs/Tansy Bellwether|Tansy Bellwether]]** is the hostess who stays with the party and calls for help. See [[NPCs/Groups/Sugarbend Fairies|the fairy roster]] for workers and spare names. Names and scene assignments are session preparation.

## The elder's priorities and understanding

The elder is balancing the needs of the working community, but **the needs of the Umbral buyers take priority**. Keeping those buyers supplied is his foremost concern during the shortage. He has been cutting everyone in town’s Sweetpitch rations to put more toward Umbral demand. The local population is barely getting by on those reduced portions. Exact amounts remain undefined; even these cuts have not cleared the backlog.

He and the other fairies know very little about the material world. They are visitors who happened across a valuable, addictive resource, rather than established locals with a broad understanding of the region. Their experience collecting and trading the pond's product does not give them expertise in the old woman's machinery, local institutions, or the intentions of material-world inhabitants.

He is well behind on supplying the Umbral buyers, in a bind, and looking to make a deal. His confirmed opening offer is to pay the party to visit the Umbral buyers and negotiate more time. Their presence can demonstrate that he is taking action. The bargain, any payment, and how he presents the party remain to be developed. It should follow from his buyer-first priorities and limited understanding. Do not assume he recognizes their affiliations, knows the Electric Woman job brought them into the area, or understands what help they could provide until that information reaches him in play.

## Bargaining goods

The elder does not offer gold; that currency has no meaning to the fairies. Umbral currency remains undefined. The fairies will not distribute Waycaps to non-Umbral creatures as goods or rewards. Oren’s desperate, personally guided crossing remains an exceptional use, not permission to give the party a supply. His opening offer includes **10 Pondstrider potions for the party**, each a single dose. Delivery timing for the Pondstrider reward remains to be agreed. The offer is preparation, not a recorded transfer. It is distinct from Sweetpitch and the nectar banquet.

## Secrecy and the illegal crossing

The fairies are deeply reluctant to discuss the Umbral with outsiders. Only extreme pressure from the current crisis makes the elder consider disclosure and bringing the party across. He knows that bringing them is against the law. The fairies are already on the wrong side of the law, and he expects the people waiting on the other side to overlook this further violation if it fixes their problems. This is his expectation, not a guarantee of immunity or an agreed response from every authority. The precise law, existing offenses, enforcement, and identities involved remain undefined.

He gives only information necessary for the proposed job and journey. Local history does not become a lecture about the Umbral. He withholds its society, geography, politics, and other inhabitants; a question or successful social roll does not automatically unlock an exposition dump. His secrecy and conscious legal risk are separate from the Waycaps: he still sincerely expects their physical crossing to work.

## Mushrooms and travel between planes

The fairies call their traveling mushrooms **Waycaps** (singular: **waycap**). They use them to "trip" in a literal sense: the mushrooms allow them to journey between planes of existence. This is a means of travel, not merely a hallucination or a deliberately constructed dream prison.

The elder expects the mushrooms to work on the party and has no reason to think otherwise. He has never heard of anyone but fairies using them, but does not interpret that lack of precedent as a reason for doubt. He is extending familiar experience to unfamiliar visitors, not knowingly proposing an experiment he considers risky. He does not know the actual difference in how the mushrooms affect material-plane creatures.

**DM mechanism:** for fairies, the mushrooms transport both soul and body to the Umbral. For material-plane creatures such as the party, they only partly work: the soul detaches from the body and moves into a purgatory, while the body remains behind on the material plane.

The party enters **the same purgatory**, which takes on either Umbral or Effulgent properties for the separated groups. Aulberos and Vivatrix experience the Umbral aspect; Aelar and Chein experience the Effulgent aspect. Naieht is fully present in one aspect, chosen before play; either side works with the current trip guide. All five bodies remain behind on the material plane.

The later design direction replaces Naieht’s ghost-between-both role. The [[Sessions/Potential Events/The Two Truths v2 - Trip DM Run Guide|trip run guide]] provides the crystal arch, moving bridge, seasonal garden, and a proposed route back to the bodies. Its communication and puzzle rules are encounter preparation, not universal planar laws. Exact elapsed time, companion travel effects, and numerical treatment effects remain open.

**Agreed scene preparation:** the elder offers to lead the party himself and asks everyone traveling to eat their mushrooms together so they can follow him. He advises against traveling alone. He crosses bodily to the Umbral, waits about a minute when the party does not arrive, and returns to find them on the ground, somewhat unconscious. They hear him say something is wrong, then another fairy who is with them calls for someone to fetch the village doctor/herbalist. The elder and doctor assume accidental poisoning and do not know the party's souls traveled. The doctor uses healing medicines and magic to stabilize them, criticizes the elder, and is not especially concerned if the strangers ultimately die. Their identity, exact medicines, spells, and mechanical treatment effects remain open. While the party is absent, their bodies are moved to Sugarbend's version of a clinic; they come to there upon returning. The clinic does not itself establish the means of their souls' return. This is planned scene material, not an event already recorded in play.

These mushrooms are distinct from the addictive sugar produced in the pond. No shared organism or process has been established. The Umbral/Effulgent properties belong to the purgatory experience, not to the biological/alchemical production of sugar. V1's deliberate dream prison, fixed delay, and guaranteed harmless return do not carry over automatically.

## History

The first fairies drank the ordinary pond water and kept returning for it. Only later did they discover the richer bottom layer, the sweetwaters. Later still, they learned to turn sweetwaters into Sweetpitch through settling and fire magic. These were separate stages of discovery, not one founding breakthrough. Sugarbend has now been an established village for many years. Access to the sugar has developed into local dependence and an established trade that has spread dependence into the Umbral. Only somewhat recently did the fairies connect their supply with the old woman's operation and her adding sugar to the water. They have been studying her operation from afar.

The fairies have collected some sugar cane themselves. It does **not** have the same properties as the sugar they extract from the water. Access to the crop alone therefore does not replace their supply: they need her process to continue. They know the two products differ. The transformation actually occurs in the pond, through the biological/alchemical activity described below; how much the fairies understand of that mechanism remains open.

The first crossing, exact dates, discoverers, and the timing of the village’s founding relative to each discovery remain open. Its age is established broadly: many years. The earlier suggestion that ancestors were trapped here after the last Umbral age is not part of this history.

## The process — accidental alchemy in the pond

Something living in the pond—a mold, algae, or similar organism—transforms the incoming sugars through a biological/alchemical process. The specific organism and details of the reaction remain undecided.

The old woman's operation supplies the sugary water. The pond organism performs the transformation that gives the collected product its distinctive effects. The fairies then collect and extract that product for consumption and trade. Raw cane has not undergone this transformation, which explains why their collected cane does not have the same properties.

This process does **not** use, extract, separate, or concentrate Umbral/Effulgent energy. Whether the old woman's theory about moving souls is correct remains unresolved. The pond's biological/alchemical transformation does not prove or disprove her explanation of the machinery.

The current supply depends on her continued runoff feeding the pond process. This does not establish that her machinery is intrinsically irreplaceable: whether the fairies could feed or cultivate the organism themselves is still an open discovery, not something they already know how to do. Their understanding of the pond organism remains unconfirmed. The old woman is unaware of the downstream collection and addictive product.

## Sweetwaters and the finished product

### Established history and process

The first fairies simply drank the pond water. It still contains some of the sugars, but drinking it is no longer the usual way they consume the product. After a period of repeated visits for ordinary pond water, they discovered a richer layer settling along the bottom, which they call **the sweetwaters**. The process of making Sweetpitch from it was discovered later still.

1. **Collect:** extract the sweetwaters from the bottom layer, rather than gathering ordinary surface water. Workers use the Pondstrider described below to walk down to this layer; the exact collecting vessels remain proposed.
2. **Settle:** leave the collected sweetwaters in large barrels, where they separate further.
3. **Draw:** take the resulting high-sugar-content water from the barrels.
4. **Flash:** blast this concentrate with fire magic, flashing off the water.
5. **Gather:** collect the remaining **black, tar-like molasses**. This is **Sweetpitch**, the addictive product normally consumed and traded.

The pond’s biological/alchemical process creates the distinctive product; the village’s work separates and concentrates it. This is the established fictional behavior of the sweetwaters, not a claim about ordinary dissolved sugar settling out of water. Exact organism, reaction, timing, yields, consumption method, and game effects remain open. Nectar served at the banquet and Waycaps remain separate substances.

### Pondstrider

**Established effect:** drinking this potion allows a creature to walk and breathe underwater as though on land. It can fight underwater without the disadvantages normally imposed by underwater fighting. **It cannot swim while the potion is active.** This replaces the proposed clinging salve and Spider Climb effect; it is a drink, not something applied to the feet.

The fairies use it to walk along the pond bottom and reach the sweetwaters. It lets them work submerged rather than collecting from the surface. It does not establish the ability to walk on the water’s surface, climb walls, or fly underwater.

**Duration and dose:** Pondstrider lasts until the drinker’s next long rest. Each potion is a single dose for one drinker.

**Still to define:** available quantities, activation timing, whether it can end early, and what happens if the drinker loses contact with the bottom. Do not silently inherit the previously proposed salve’s one-hour duration or other mechanics. The potion’s effects on currents, visibility, pressure, and other environmental hazards have not been specified.

**Proposed collection action:** workers carry stoppered pots down into the pond, open them within the sweetwaters just above the mud, and seal them before walking back to shore. They pour their collections into the large settling barrels. This replaces the earlier proposed cord-operated bottom-draw pots.

**Proposed barrel arrangement:** the rich liquid forms a lower band above any settled pond grit. A low tap draws that band; a higher tap removes the thinner water above. Workers check the layers rather than emptying the entire barrel into the finishing vessel. Layer positions and apparatus are draft implementation details, not additional confirmed chemistry.

**Proposed finishing station:** shallow heatproof vessels sit on stone stands away from the mushroom homes. A fire-worker flashes off the water, then other workers scrape the black residue into storage pots. The barrels are large relative to the fairies; their dimensions and the fairies’ exact height remain unset.

### Sweetpitch

**Sweetpitch** is the fairy name for the finished addictive product: the black, tar-like molasses left after the water is flashed off. The unprocessed bottom layer is still called **sweetwaters**; raw cane sugar, the banquet nectar, and Waycaps are distinct.

## What the woman knows

The woman and her daughters squeeze sugarwater out of the cane because the squeezed cane burns very hot and makes good fuel for their steam engine. They discard the sugarwater. She does not know the sugars collect downstream at all, let alone that a pond organism transforms them into an addictive product. The fairies' supply is an accidental byproduct of fuel preparation, not something she knowingly produces for them.

## Current crisis and attacks

**Dissent:** Rusk Thornwake fears the buyers’ dwindling patience and advocates attacking the woman’s hut to take control of the operation. He is close to organizing action without Oren’s authority. See [[NPCs/Groups/Sugarbend Fairies#Rusk Thornwake — the dissenter|Rusk’s portrayal]]. No new attack, organized following, or involvement in earlier attacks is established.

**The town’s prevailing mood is desperation.** Everyone’s Sweetpitch ration has been cut to supply the Umbral buyers. Dependence, dwindling portions, and repeated sacrifice for distant demand hang over ordinary work and conversation. The community is still functioning, but only just. This is established background, not a possible future consequence.

For portrayal, show strained concentration, exhausted patience, people watching the portioning rather than their own work, and careful efforts to recover every trace from a vessel. These are suggested scene cues, not new withdrawal mechanics. Keep residents individual: some complain, some go quiet, some keep helping a neighbor. Public complaints refer to “shipments” or “what is being sent away,” preserving the fairies’ reluctance to discuss the Umbral.

The shortage is already happening. Sugarbend is dealing with depleted reserves, scarcity, and a population that has not had its fix. Keeping the situation under control is an immediate struggle, not a concern about a possible future shortage.

**Fairy bands have attacked the electric factory exactly twice, both times during extended vacations taken by the woman and her daughters.** These attacks are established background events. They do not by themselves establish a coordinated village plan or the elder's authorization. The bands' identities, precise aims, damage, casualties, and the old woman's responses have not yet been specified.

The fairies need the old woman's process to keep working; attacks that damage it could worsen the shortage. The existing scarcity has two established pressures: the equipment has been running less, while the number of sugar addicts keeps growing. Reduced output is coincidental: the woman and her daughters take vacations, perform routine maintenance, or study things that do not require the equipment. They do not know of the fairies beyond the two attacks, and have no knowledge of their dependence, collection, or trade. The attacks happened during existing extended vacations; they are not the reason those breaks began. No special equipment fault or deliberate cutoff is established. Specific repercussions in the Umbral remain open.

The revised encounter begins amid this ongoing crisis and prior violence. It does not begin with the first possibility of an attack. Whether the village chooses negotiation, another attack, or a plan involving the party remains to be developed in [[Sessions/Potential Events/The Two Truths v2|The Two Truths v2]].
