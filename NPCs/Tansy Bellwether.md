**Race/Build:** Fae of [[Sugarbend]] — small, round, warm, with dusty moth-wings folded like a shawl and a scatter of tiny brass bells stitched along the rims of her ears
**Age:** Old by mortal counting, young by [[Sugarbend]]'s
**Role:** The hostess of the Long Supper. Serves the mushrooms, then stays behind to sit with the sleepers while everyone else goes upriver.

**Appearance**
- Apron always dusted with sugar crystals that catch the lantern light and never seem to fall off
- Hands that are never still — pouring, folding, tucking a napkin — and, if you watch closely, faintly shaky
- The little ear-bells chime when she turns her head, so she can't sneak up on anyone and has clearly made peace with that
- A lovely smile that reaches her eyes, and dark circles under them that it doesn't quite hide

**Personality**
- Genuinely loves feeding people. The hospitality is real. That's what makes the rest of it awful.
- Never lies outright. Says true things in an order that leaves out the timing.
- Guilty in a way she has already talked herself into being okay with: the pond is thinning, people are getting mean, and *somebody* has to keep the guests comfortable.
- Sugar-hungry herself. She stays behind because she drew the short straw, and it shows.

**Voice & Sample Lines**

> "Sit, sit — you've come a long way to be hungry, and Sugarbend doesn't send guests to bed wanting."

> "Violet for the sweet dreamers, grey for the ones who take their tea bitter. Nobody's ever been wrong about which one they wanted."

> "It's only a dream, dears. A long one. It's the kindest thing we've got."

> *(on waking, without looking at them)* "I never said it wouldn't take a while."

**Running Her**
- **At the feast:** warm, quick, always in motion. She's the one handing out the caps and she chooses who gets which without explanation. Drop small honest-sounding lines that pay off later (the pond's "a bit thin lately"; "half the village is helping with the harvest").
- **On waking:** she's the only one left, sitting a careful distance from the sleepers with a cup of sugar-water she isn't drinking. She doesn't run and she doesn't fight. If pressed she tells them most of it: the pond has been thinning for weeks, the folk are getting mean, they've gone to take the mill, *nobody's meant to get hurt if the Old Woman lets them.* See [[The Two Truths#Waking — The Conflict Upriver]].
- She is **not** an enemy. If the party turns on her she folds instantly and points upriver.
