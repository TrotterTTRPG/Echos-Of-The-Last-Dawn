# Sugarbend Fairies

Names and light portrayal cues for [[Sugarbend]] and [[Sessions/Session Notes/009/Outline|session 009]]. These assignments are preparation, not additional events already played. No combat statistics or new magical abilities are established here.

## Main cast

| Name | Role | Recognition and delivery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oren Reedwick** | The elder | A neatly fastened reed-fiber coat; keeps straightening its cuffs while listening. Speaks carefully and pauses before disclosing anything sensitive. |
| **Rusk Thornwake** | Dissenter advocating seizure of the hut | Keeps his shaking hands clasped behind his back. Speaks softly enough that others must stop talking to hear him. His fear of the buyers makes delay intolerable. |
| **Vetch Bitterleaf** | Village doctor / herbalist | Rolled sleeves and herb-stained fingertips. Gives instructions while continuing to work. Criticizes Oren’s decision without much concern for the strangers’ survival. |
| **[[NPCs/Tansy Bellwether|Tansy Bellwether]]** | Hostess; fairy who stays with the group | Dusty moth-wings and little ear-bells. Practical, attentive hospitality. She calls for the herbalist when the crossing fails. |

**Shared pressure:** everyone in town has had their Sweetpitch ration cut to supply the Umbral buyers. Work continues under deprivation and strain. Use different responses—irritability, silence, fixation, or trying to support another worker—rather than playing every resident identically.

**Knowledge and motives:** Oren prioritizes the Umbral buyers and offers the illegal journey only under extreme pressure. All the fairies guard information about the Umbral. Tansy is not knowingly trapping the party. Oren and Vetch initially assume accidental poisoning, not soul travel. Vetch has used medicines and healing magic to stabilize them; exact treatments remain undefined.

## Rusk Thornwake — the dissenter

**Role:** a desperate male fairy in Sugarbend, pressing Oren to attack the electric woman’s hut and take control of the operation. Naming and portrayal details are prepared material; his motivation and position follow the DM’s direction.

**What drives him:** fear of what happens when the Umbral buyers stop tolerating missed shipments. Sugarbend is already on thin ice with them. Reduced rations are keeping the shipments moving without catching up; he sees this as spending the town’s remaining strength on a plan that is failing.

**His argument:** control the source so the village can keep it running. He wants an organized seizure, with the equipment intact, rather than another uncontrolled attack. He does not know how to operate it. If challenged, his proposed answer is to make the woman show them. This is his dangerous plan, not proof that coercion would produce reliable output.

**Where he approaches treason:** he still addresses Oren as elder and initially demands that Oren authorize action. But he is beginning to ask whether those willing to act should need permission. His next step would be gathering volunteers behind Oren’s back. No rebellion, recruited force, departure time, or new attack is already established. His involvement in the previous two attacks remains undecided.

**How to play him:** controlled until someone calls for patience. His hands shake; he hides them rather than making a display of suffering. He watches the depleted workers while arguing. He fears consequences for the whole town, even while proposing a solution that could make everything worse. He can be persuaded by a concrete, credible way to secure supply or more time; reassurance alone will not satisfy him.

**What he will not disclose:** he is as guarded about the Umbral as the others. Around the party, he says “the buyers” or “the people waiting.” He does not name their powers, laws, identities, or punishments. Fear is enough; no specific reprisal has been selected.

### Lines to use

- **To Oren:** “We have cut everyone’s share. We are still behind. Tell me what gets better if we wait.”
- **His proposal:** “Take the hut. Keep the works whole. She shows us how it runs, and we keep it running.”
- **When challenged about the machinery:** “Then she teaches us. I did not say burn it down.”
- **On the buyers:** “They have already waited longer than they meant to. Do you want to find out when they stop?”
- **At the edge of defiance:** “If you will not give the order, how long do you expect the rest of us to stand here?”
- **To the party, if they offer an alternative:** “Tell me what you can actually change. Not what you hope she will agree to.”

**Pressure, not a forced encounter:** use his dissent to give the party something to answer. If they engage with a plausible plan, he can listen, demand evidence, or provisionally wait. Declining the adventure does not automatically launch his attack. Decide escalation from actual play.
