Location: Outside [[The Deephold]] / [[The Bronze Bastion]], Bronze District, [[Thalyra]]
Central NPC: [[Corwin Sallow]] ("Corry," "the Devil-Killer")
Type: Roleplay scene + investigation hook. Curdles from comedy into horror. No forced combat at the scene.
Seeds: [[Velrosk]]-bound crate / the return of *[[Mella]]*. Pullable immediately; if ignored, it comes back for the party later.

> *Flavor lifted from the song "Conditions of My Parole" — a paroled drunk swears he killed the devil, not a woman, and is terrified to be set free. In this world the devil is real, and her name is [[Mella]].*

---

## The Scene — A Man Who Won't Leave

**Narrator: (Jon)**

The party is passing through the Bronze District near [[The Bronze Bastion]] when they hear the commotion before they see it: shouting, the scrape of boots on stone, and a man's voice cracking with panic.

Two [[The Watchful Wardens of Thalyra|Wardens]] are hauling a gaunt, sallow man out the side door of [[The Deephold]] by the arms. He is not resisting like a prisoner — he is resisting like a man being pushed *off* the boat. He claws back toward the doorway. He hooks his fingers on the frame. One Warden pries them loose; the other curses and shoves.

> *"No — NO — you can't, you don't understand, put me back, I'll do anything, I'll confess to ANYTHING, just put me back behind the stone—!"*

The Wardens are past patience. *"Sentence is served, Sallow. We are not running an inn."* They mean to dump him in the gutter and be done with it. 

This display goes on - the guards are trying to be gentle to this old man, but he keeps throwing himself at the door, or getting an arm inside.

If the party engages:

> *"You. You lot look like you've seen things. Tell them. Tell them a man can be right and ruined at the same time. Don't let them put me out in the dark."*

**The Wardens' position (if questioned):**
- He's a known drunk, in and out of the upper cells for years. Public intoxication, public urination, parole violation — petty stuff.
- A few months back there was "some business" — blood on a floor, a story about a murdered woman, no body, no victim, no proof any woman ever lived there. The magistrate couldn't make anything stick.
- "He *wanted* to be locked up. Kept breaking parole on purpose so we'd take him back in. We're done playing along."
- They genuinely do not care where he goes. They'll let the party take responsibility for him with visible relief.

## His Tale — The Comedy That Curdles

Corry tells it the way he's told it a hundred times: loud, slurring, grinning, performing. Let the table laugh first. The horror is in the details that hold up.

**His patter (paraphrase / quotable lines):**
- *"Sweet baby Tyr on the scales, I need an advocate and a miracle to get my ass out of this."*
- *"The devil kept at me — so I shipped her off to Velrosk, 'cause I was over it."*
- *"Hand on the Scales of Tyr — I killed the devil, not some bitch. But the Wardens don't give a shit."*
- *"Show a little mercy. I've been straight and narrow since the judge and the warden paroled me."*
- *"They couldn't find a body to hang me with. Couldn't corroborate my 'bullshit story.'"*
- *"Don't let them release me. What if she's worse than dead — and comes to finish what she started?"*

**The story he tells** (see [[Corwin Sallow]] for the full version):
There was a woman named *[[Mella]]*. She showed up to my house one night. It was cold and wet. I asked her why she was out in the cold, but she was shy. I invited her in. I gave her a spot in the attic to sleep. She was mighty happy for my kindness. She became... more than friendly. Mella never talked, but when the night came she had her own language to tell me how happy she was having a home. Then one night things changed. She she came into my room as she had been doing, and things progressed naturally. But this time something came out of her mouth and latched onto my arm! She held me down with the strength of 10 men! With that thing, she drank and and drank and I screamed - but noone was around to hear me. I thought I was dead but when she was done - she didn't kill me, obviously- she didn't even try to hurt me. She just  left the room. I found her up in the attic hours later when I gained some of my strength back. I had brought my crossbow with me and I shot her through the heart with a bolt. She dropped dead and I buried her before the sun came up. All the next day I worried. What if I was just drunk, and I'm not remembering things right. That night she came back. She was clean, and gentle. She still wanted me. I convinced myself that I had dreamed it. But I hadn't. She feasted again, and I killed her again. She always came back  — night after night, for months. I could help myself but to let her in. Months of her feeding wasting me to a husk. So I stuck her in a freight crate, and put a weeks pay worth of nails to seal it. I shipped her to [[Velrosk]]. It bought me three quiet nights, but she came back anyway. 

I got himself arrested because a stone cell is the one place she's never reached him.

### The "tells" — quiet corroboration

Let the players investigate the lunatic. Every check that should debunk him instead deepens the dread.

- **DC 12 Wisdom (Insight)** — He is not lying, and he is not performing the fear. Under the drunk-theater is a man who has not slept properly in months and is genuinely, soberly terrified. He believes his story - true or not. 
- **DC 13 Wisdom (Medicine)** — He has a faint circular scar on his arm. It looks like it's been healed for a long time, or has had healing magics applied to it.
- **DC 25 Intelligence (Religion) or (Arcana)** — The lore he rattles off sounds close to depictions of undead lore. But nothing you know of really matches the description.
### The damning counter-evidence (keep the ambiguity alive)

If the party leans toward believing him, let the doubt back in:
- There is **no record of [[Mella]].** No lease, no name in the district rolls, no neighbor who'll vouch she existed. 
- The attic-corner he points to is empty and, by every account but his, always was.
- He's a chronic drunk with a documented history. The simplest story is the ugly mundane one: a man killed someone — or no one — in a blackout and built a monster to live in the hole where the memory should be.

**The hook is the tension.** The party leaves not knowing whether they're looking at a delusional wretch or a man telling the truth. *(DM note: he is telling the truth. [[Mella]] is real. See below.)*

## What He Wants / What He Carries

- He wants **sanctuary.** Locked up, warded, watched, *never in the dark.* Failing that, he wants the party to keep him alive — or to finish [[Mella]] for good.
- On his person: the well-worn **hand crossbow** he's shot Mella through the heart with twice, and a half-empty case of bolts; a creased **[[Velrosk]] freight chit** (the crate's manifest stub, the clerk's mark smudged); a tin **token of [[Tyr]]**; an empty gin bottle he won't surrender.
- The freight chit is the loose thread. It names a crate, a Velrosk-bound run, and a clerk at [[Merchant's Landing - Western Docks]].

## Pulling the Thread (Immediate)

If the party engages, three live leads branch off the scene. Any one of them confirms the truth and points at the climax.

**1. The Docks — the freight chit.** At [[Merchant's Landing - Western Docks]], the manifest clerk (bribable, nervous) confirms a crate *was* logged on a Velrosk run weeks ago — "heavy, stank, the drunk paid in silver and gin, told me not to open it."
- **DC 13 Investigation** of the dock records: the outbound vessel's return manifest notes the crate's lashings were found *burst from the inside* mid-voyage, and the ship lost two of crew to "fever" — found bloodless in their hammocks. The crate came back to Thalyra **empty.**
- Translation for the DM: [[Mella]] worked the stake loose at sea, fed, and has already made landfall. She is in or near the city, and she is hunting back toward Corry.

**2. The Attic — the lodging house.** The Bronze District room Corry names is real, cheap, and "unrented."
- **DC 12 Investigation**: a scrubbed-but-not-clean bloodstain soaked deep into the floorboards; nail-scars on the inside of the window frame; the boards of one corner pried up and re-laid — *a small grave opened from beneath.*
- **DC 14 Survival / Arcana**: an unnatural cold patch that holds even at midday; no rot where rot should be. Something dead has lain here and risen, more than once.
- No paper trail for "[[Mella]]" — because she never needed one.

**3. Guard the Bait — Corry himself.** The simplest, most dangerous play: keep Corry close and wait. He is right that she comes for *him.* The first night he's outside stone walls, she finds him. (Go to the climax.)

## The Climax — [[Mella]] Returns

**[[Mella]]** — see her file for the full stat block, traits, and actions (custom undead, CR 6, not a standard vampire).

***Siphon.*** A long, leech-like tongue uncoils from her mouth and fastens onto exposed skin — an arm, a wrist, the soft of the throat — leaving a ring-shaped welt, never a fang-mark. This is *why* no examiner ever flagged Corry as a vampire's victim.

**Running her against 5 level-4 PCs:** at CR 6, she's a **deadly** fight at this tier on her own — which is correct for a climax, but lean on the levers below so it's survivable and dramatic rather than a grind.

***Her Devotion (the opening Corry swears by).*** [[Mella]] will not willingly reduce **Corry** to 0 HP, and she would rather be destroyed than truly lose him. Exploitable:
- She always moves toward Corry and prioritizes reaching him over fighting the party.
- If Corry speaks to her, pleads, or steps into her path (his action), she hesitates: the next attack against her before her next turn has **advantage.**
- If the party uses Corry as a shield or threatens to harm him, she falters and may parley — she wants him *fed upon and kept,* not corpsed. This is the "her love kept leaving me openings" he keeps repeating, made mechanical.
- Her **Siphon** can only target Corry — no one else will do. A single hit barely scratches him (1d4) but heals her for ten times that, and the instant she feeds, the gauntness melts away and she looks fully human again. The party needs to keep her off him, not just survive her.
- Watch for **Hungering Gaze**: a battlefield-control trick, not a feeding tool — it never targets Corry. A PC who fails the save is enthralled and rolls each turn to see what they actually do: drawn helplessly toward Mella, frozen and useless, or lashing out at the nearest ally. It can turn a PC into a liability for a round or more without putting Corry at any more risk than he's already in.

**She is not a standard vampire** — the old hunter's tricks mostly don't apply. **Sunlight hypersensitivity** is her one reliable weakness (hold her till dawn, or force her into direct light). Running water, a wooden weapon to the heart, and the need for an invitation are *not* guaranteed counters anymore — the crossbow bolts Corry's put through her heart only ever stilled her, never killed her, which is consistent with how she keeps coming back.

**Outcomes:**
- *Destroyed properly* (staked in her resting place, or burned, or left to the sun): Corry is finally free. He may weep with relief, or fall apart — she was, in his ruined way, loved.
- *Driven off but not destroyed:* she retreats to her resting place to heal (1 hour) and the hunt resumes. The party now knows what they're dealing with.
- *Corry dies:* she has no reason to linger near the party — and Thalyra has a free, hungry undead with no anchor and almost no weakness anyone can name.

## If the Party Walks Away (Pull It Back Later)

Corry is not a quest-giver they can fail without consequence — he's a fuse.
- If they refuse him at the scene, the Wardens turn him loose anyway. Within a few nights he turns up **drained and dead** in a Bronze District gutter (the welts on his arm finally explained), or simply **vanishes** — his last act on record being that he grabbed *this party* and begged.
- Either way, [[Mella]] is now in Thalyra with her anchor gone and her hunger intact. Bloodless bodies begin appearing — dock crew, drunks, the overlooked. Reintroduce her as a city-level problem the party half-recognizes: *"Tyr's scales… it was that man outside the Deephold. He told us."*
- The [[Velrosk]] thread remains pullable indefinitely: the crate, the clerk, the burst lashings, the dead ship's crew.

## Hooks & Rewards

- **Docket potential:** if the party can't or won't act privately, the bloodless-corpse pattern is exactly the kind of thing that lands on [[The Adventurer's Docket]] — quietly, before it can spook the Gilded city. A patron who wants it handled *discreetly* is a clean way to make this an official job.
- **Reward (if run as a job):** ~150–250 gp bounty for resolving the "Bronze District blood-killings," plus whatever the grateful (or terrified) Corry can scrape together — which is nothing but his crossbow, his story, and his thanks.
- **Reputation:** the Wardens quietly owe the party for cleaning up a mess they laughed off. Good leverage with [[Captain Seressa Varn]], who has no love for being made to look careless.
