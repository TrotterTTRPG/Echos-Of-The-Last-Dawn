## Room 2 — The Threshold

**The physical space (same for both):** a garden gate blocking the only way forward. Set into its face is a **ring of four symbols**, arranged like the points of a compass. Beneath them, a four-line rhyme, **half legible on each side.**

**The rhyme (DM only — full text):**
> *First, the light that wakes the seed;*
> *then, the warmth that feeds the need;*
> *then, the hush that dims the day;*
> *last, the dark where lost things stay.*

**The order:** **Dawn → Noon → Dusk → Midnight.**

**The ring (clockwise from the top, as the party faces the gate):**

| Position | Meaning | Effulgent sees | Umbral sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top** | **Noon** | A wide-open golden bloom, round as a coin | A full sun-disc with straight rays |
| **Right** | **Midnight** | A dark violet night-flower with a silver heart, closed at the edges | A solid black disc, no rays |
| **Bottom** | **Dawn** | A pale gold bud, half-open, petals folded like praying hands | A half-sun sitting on a line, short rays fanning up |
| **Left** | **Dusk** | A rose-colored bloom, petals folding shut | A half-sun sinking below a line, rays fanning up |

The ring is deliberately **not** in cyclic order (clockwise from the top it reads Noon, Midnight, Dawn, Dusk), so the sequence isn't obvious from the layout.

**Who can read what:**
- **Effulgent** can read lines 1 and 3 (*"First, the light that wakes the seed"* and *"then, the hush that dims the day"*). They can match the bud (Dawn) and the folding bloom (Dusk).
- **Umbral** can read lines 2 and 4 (*"then, the warmth that feeds the need"* and *"last, the dark where lost things stay"*). They can match the full sun (Noon) and the black disc (Midnight).
- **Neither table alone has the order.** Effulgent knows Dawn comes before Dusk but not where Noon and Midnight slot in; Umbral knows Midnight is last but not what leads to Noon. Together it's clean.
- **Umbral's two half-suns look almost identical.** They can only tell which is Dawn and which is Dusk by asking Effulgent which *position* their bud and their folding bloom sit in.

**The puzzle:** touch all four in order. **The gate opens when *both* tables have completed the sequence.** A wrong touch makes the ring **dim and reset** with a dull *clunk* the other table can hear through the wall. That sound is the tutorial: *you need the other room, and they need you.*

**Solution:** Bottom → Top → Left → Right (Dawn, Noon, Dusk, Midnight). Both tables do it.

## Room 3 — The Tapestry and the Door

**The physical space:** a wall blocking further progress. **Effulgent** sees a heavy, beautiful **tapestry** of the fae court dancing. **Umbral** sees a **broken door**, half off its hinges, in the same wall.

**The rule that makes it a puzzle:** **the tapestry has no passage behind it until the Umbral group moves the door.**
- If **Effulgent** takes the tapestry down (it's just hung on hooks — no lock, no trap), they find **only bare honey-stone wall.** Nothing. That's the intended dead end, and the moment they realize they can't solve this one alone.
- If **Umbral** forces the broken door open (it's wedged and warped, not locked — a shove or a plain Athletics check, DC 10, no penalty for failing except noise), the passage is behind it. **At that moment, Effulgent's tapestry billows out on a sudden draft and there's an archway where there wasn't one.** Both tables now have a way forward.

**The "aha":** the Umbral table sees a way forward immediately. The Effulgent table sees only a wall. The puzzle is that **Effulgent has to figure out the way through is on the other side, and Umbral has to realize the other room can't do it without them.** Let them talk it out. Someone shouting "there's a door over here" and the other side answering "we've got a tapestry there" is the whole scene.

**Sound cues:** when Umbral forces the door, Effulgent hears a sharp crack of wood, and the tapestry moves. If Effulgent pulls the tapestry down first, Umbral hears a heavy fall of cloth and the door judders in its frame.

**Keep it short.** It's an insight, not an obstacle. If they solve it in fifteen minutes, that's the design working.

## Room 4 — The Herd

**The physical space:** a wide meadow crossed by the path forward, with **six creatures** spread across it and **one more figure** — the **Herder** — sitting on a stool where the path enters. The meadow has six **landmarks**; each creature stands at one.
- **Effulgent** sees a **flock of sheep** and a **kindly old shepherd.**
- **Umbral** sees a **band of redcaps** and an **old redcap graybeard.** (Nobody fights. See below.)

They are **the same creatures and the same landmarks,** seen two ways. The tables should figure this out from the landmarks and the way each creature is behaving — the sheep drinking at the pond is the redcap washing its cap in the pond. The **Herder** poses each table three riddles. Every riddle turns on a detail **only the other table can see.**

**No combat, ever.** The redcaps are bound by the fae *guest-game*: they'll glare, snarl, and grin, but they won't touch anyone. If a player attacks, the whole herd bolts and reforms in a minute or so, costing time and nothing else. If a player asks why they aren't attacking, the old redcap says, with a rusted grin: *"It's a game, love. We're all so good at games."*

**Landmarks (same for both tables — each DM dresses them their own way):**

| Landmark | Where (facing north from the path) |
|---|---|
| **Fence** — a broken rail fence | near left |
| **Pond** — a shallow pond | near right |
| **Flat Stone** — a big flat rock | center |
| **Willow** — an old drooping tree | far left |
| **Cart** — an overturned hay cart | far right |
| **Bell Post** — a tall post with a hanging bell | far center, beside the exit path |

**The Herd Key (both DMs):**

| At | Effulgent: sheep | Umbral: redcap |
|---|---|---|
| **Willow** | **Black** fleece · **brass bell** · dozing | Cap: **dried-blood brown** · **sickle** · **leather** boots · asleep against the trunk, snoring |
| **Stone** | **White** fleece · **blue ribbon** on left ear · chewing | Cap: **bright poppy-red** · **cleaver** · **iron** boots · gnawing something |
| **Fence** | **Grey** fleece · **crown of poppies** · staring steadily at the party | Cap: **deep crimson** · **cudgel** · **leather** boots · staring steadily at the party |
| **Pond** | **Cream** fleece · nothing worn · drinking | Cap: **black-red, soaked** · **nothing in hand** · **barefoot** · washing its cap in the water (it runs red) |
| **Cart** | **Dark brown** fleece · **tin bell** · lying in the cart's shade | Cap: **faded pink, sun-bleached** · **hammer** · **iron** boots · lying under the cart, picking its teeth |
| **Bell Post** | **Piebald** (grey-and-white) fleece · **yellow ribbon** round its neck · standing by the post | Cap: **plain cherry-red** · **billhook** · **iron** boots · standing by the post |

**Derived orderings (for judging answers):**
- **Cap depth, deepest → palest:** Pond, Willow, Fence, Bell Post, Stone, Cart.
- **Fleece darkness, darkest → palest:** Willow, Cart, Fence, Bell Post, Pond, Stone.
- **Bells:** Willow (brass), Cart (tin). **Ribbons:** Stone (blue), Bell Post (yellow). **Iron boots:** Stone, Cart, Bell Post.

**The riddles.** Deliver each in the Herder's voice. If a table is stuck, give the **plain version** after a wrong answer or a long silence.

**Effulgent's riddles** (the Shepherd's — need **Umbral's** eyes):

| # | Riddle (verse) | Plain version | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | *"Wool's the half of any sheep; the rest is hid where wool runs deep. Every one of mine wears red — some worn pale, some worn dead. Point me to the deepest-dyed, the crown that's soaked the most inside."* | Each sheep has a red cap under its wool. Which cap is the **deepest, darkest red?** | **Pond** |
| **E2** | *"Two of mine will ring as they roam; one of the two goes iron-shod home. Not the one that treads on leather — bring me the one with bell and iron, together."* | Two sheep wear bells. Only one of those two has **iron boots.** Which? | **Cart** *(Willow's bell-wearer has leather boots)* |
| **E3** | *"Now line them up, from deepest hue to palest rag — the whole flock through. Name each by the place they stand, and I'll open the gate by hand."* | Put the whole flock in order, **deepest cap to palest.** | **Pond, Willow, Fence, Bell Post, Stone, Cart** |

**Umbral's riddles** (the old redcap's — need **Effulgent's** eyes):

| # | Riddle (verse) | Plain version | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **U1** | *"Each of us wears a second hide, stolen or grown or worn with pride. Some are dark and some are dun — which of us wears the whitest one?"* | Each redcap has a fleece-coat you can't see. Which is the **whitest?** | **Stone** |
| **U2** | *"Ribbons tied on two of ours; one is handy with a hook. Point me to the ribboned one who has the hooked blade — take a look."* | Two have ribbons. Only one of them carries a **hooked blade.** Which? | **Bell Post** *(the Willow's sickle is hooked too, but it has no ribbon)* |
| **U3** | *"Now line us up from black to white, the darkest coat to the pale and light. Name us each by where we stand, and I'll open the gate by hand."* | Put the whole band in order, **darkest fleece to palest.** | **Willow, Cart, Fence, Bell Post, Pond, Stone** |

**How it flows:**
- The tables have to **realize the two herds are the same herd** — usually by comparing landmarks and behaviors. The Effulgent shepherd's first riddle (*"each of mine wears red underneath"*) is the strongest nudge; the old redcap's first (*"each of us wears a second hide"*) is the counterpart.
- Riddles come **in order** (1, 2, 3 per table) and the two tables can run at different paces. A wrong answer earns a **hint**: the Herder tuts, restates the plain version, and adds: *"Have you asked the folk beside you what they see?"*
- A correct answer: the named creature steps forward, dips its head, and steps back.
- **Both tables finish** → the **bell at the Bell Post rings once** (audible in both rooms) and the herd trots off down the path in a line. The way forward is open.
- **Bonus round (optional stretch, ~10–15 min):** after the third riddle, the Herder says *"Now backwards!"* and asks for the same order **reversed.** No new data.

## Room 5 — The Shrine of Two Mothers

**The physical space:** each table arrives at a small grove and a raised stone altar — the **same shrine**, perceived twice.
- **Effulgent** sees a bright altar: a statue of a beautiful winged matron, doves nesting in her arms, sweet incense burning.
- **Umbral** sees a cracked altar: the same matron, now gaunt and taloned, ravens instead of doves, ash instead of incense.

Neither table gets told who she is. In the dream she is simply the **Mother of Both Halves** — the fae's old matron figure from the days of [[The Umbral Soul]]'s last generation, the one their grandmothers still invoke. If asked, nobody in the vision answers; when they wake, Tansy will only say *"the old mother"* and shrug.

**The puzzle — a call-and-response offering:**
- **Effulgent's** statue wants something **given freely and still living** — a lock of hair, a flower conjured or produced, a minor spell of light or growth, a genuine kind word spoken aloud to the statue.
- **Umbral's** statue wants something **given up** — an old grudge spoken and released, a drop of blood, a treasured but non-magical item left behind, a genuine confession.

**If a table is stuck on what to offer, there's a plain-language hint carved into the base of each altar** — worn, but legible up close. It's not a riddle; if a player asks what it means, tell them exactly what it says:
- **Effulgent's** altar: *"Bring what still lives. Bring it freely, or bring nothing at all."*
- **Umbral's** altar: *"Bring what you would be glad to lose. She keeps nothing that is not given up gladly."*

Neither offering completes the ritual alone. **Both tables must offer at the same moment** — count it down across the wall ("on three!"). Let each player choose their own; don't prescribe. This is meant to land as a character beat, not a mechanical lock-pick.

**The exit:** when both offerings land, the grove doesn't open onto a clearing. It **dims into lantern-glow** — the Hollow Bloom ends. Both DMs cue the wake-up (see below) at the same moment.

**Vivatrix-specific beats** live in [[The Two Truths - Umbral Script#Room 5 — The Shrine of Two Mothers|the Umbral script]]. They're for her alone.

## Waking — The Conflict Upriver

Both tables wake together (merge the rooms here). The feast table, the lanterns burned low, and the sky overhead **late-night dark.** *About six hours have passed.*

**Deliver the indicators in this order:**

1. **The village is nearly empty.** Plates half-cleared. A few fae children asleep in a heap under a shared blanket. The band that was playing is gone; the instruments have been left leaning against a tree.
2. **The mooring is empty.** The ferry is gone. The tracks from the afternoon are much more numerous now — hundreds of tiny prints headed upriver, some deep enough to have been running.
3. **[[Tansy Bellwether]] is the only one left,** sitting a careful distance from the sleepers with a cup of sugar-water she isn't drinking. She doesn't run. *"I never said it wouldn't take a while."*
4. **Upriver, the sky is lit.** Bluish-white flashes under a clear, starry sky, no thunder, rhythmic and *wrong.* A heavy, deliberate thud every few seconds, like a stone being dropped from a height. A smear of dark smoke across the stars. The wind carries a **thick, sweet, scorched smell** — burning sugar — and, very faintly, a bell ringing, fast and frantic.
5. **Anyone who looks closely** sees the blue-white flashes have a *pattern* — pulses, then a long gap, then a cluster. Someone's working the generator. Someone's fighting to keep it running. **Or to stop it.**

**Tansy, if pressed** (Insight DC 12 says she's not lying and not proud):
- The pond has been thinning for **weeks.** The Old Woman stopped letting the sweet run to the river.
- Folk are getting mean. She's seen it in the children.
- They've gone to **take the mill.** *"We'll run it ourselves. Somebody has to."* She's already talked herself into believing it.
- Nobody's meant to get hurt **if the Old Woman lets them.** She doesn't sound sure.
- She'll tell them the way upriver (the ferry's gone, but there's a footpath along the bank), and the names she knows: the Old Woman, and her two daughters, **Ally** and **Mary.**
- She will **not** stop them. She will not fight them. If they turn on her, she folds and points.

**Optional lever:** the trip counts as a **long rest** (they slept ~6 hours, unharmed). Grant it if the Old Woman fight is next and you want them fresh; withhold it if you want them tired and rattled.

**DM only — the state of play upriver** (use it to pace the next session; see [[Electric Woman]]):
- The fae are **many, small, fast, and hungry.** They aren't soldiers. They're a mob with ropes and good knives who believe they're owed this.
- The **Old Woman** is protecting the generator, not the fae. She's clever, and she has the strongest weapon in the valley. **Ally and Mary** are holding the mill with her.
- Every hour the party dawdles costs the Old Woman ground. Tie the scale of what they find when they get there to how quickly they go.

## DM Notes — Running Two Tables At Once

- **The single hardest part of this session is state-sync.** Agree on your signal before you start, and use it every time a room-changing action happens (door forced, riddle answered, piece placed, offering made). The signal can be small — a text saying "R3 U1 done" — but it has to be reliable.
- **Both tables should always have something to do.** Room 1 gives both groups turns guiding and drawing; Room 4 uses both groups’ observations. Rooms 2 and 3 are shorter and lean on cross-talk. If one table finishes early, the *other* is on the critical path — let them sweat, and let the finished table shout encouragement or a detail through the wall.
- **Let both rooms fail forward.** Puzzle info flows through the wall. The only "hint system" you need is the other table's ears. Hold back on nudges until someone's genuinely stuck.
- **Don't let either DM improvise a hard contradiction.** If a player asks something your script doesn't cover, keep it vague and sensory ("old, warped, heavier than it looks") rather than inventing a hard detail that might clash with the other room. This matters most in Rooms 1 and 4, where a wrong invented detail can break the key.
- **Nothing in here is a trap.** There's no failure state that costs resources. If a table is frustrated, hand them a hint. The dream's job is to be a long, lovely, occasionally maddening delay, not a wall.
- **Hidden details are the point.** Both scripts are stuffed with irrelevant texture on purpose. Don't linger on the important stuff, and don't rush past the unimportant stuff. If the players can tell from your pacing which detail matters, you're reading it wrong.

## DM Eyes Only — The Vivatrix Thread

[[Vivatrix]]'s patron, **[[The Undying]] / [[The Umbral Dragon]]**, is never named here and nothing in this session should confirm it to her player. The whole thread is atmosphere, not exposition:

- **She sits Umbral, and it should feel like coming home.** Her tablemates should be visibly unsettled by the rot, the hunger, and the wrongness. She isn't. She finds the ash-smell oddly clean, the redcaps oddly polite, the ghost oddly kind. **Never explain why.**
- Per **[[The Two Truths - Umbral Script]]**, there are private, quiet asides seeded through the room scripts — and one more on waking. They're written for her alone. **Never announce them to the table.** Deliver them as quiet personal color, the way you'd hand a player a note.
- **The old redcap's aside in Room 4** is the strongest: *"You're closer to home already, aren't you."* The Herder isn't working for anyone, and doesn't know what it's seeing. It's just a very old fae who recognizes a scent.
- **This is a seed, not a payoff.** Nothing in this session reveals the Umbral Dragon connection. The goal is that, in hindsight, this night reads as foreshadowing — and that Vivatrix's player gets to feel, for one night, what it's like to be comfortable in the dark.
