# Savone’s Test — The Courier’s Account

**Running at the table:** [[Sessions/Potential Events/Savones Test - DM Run Guide|DM Run Guide — no advance reading required]]. Includes scene instructions, branches, room cards, and condensed combat statistics.

## Quick synopsis

Savone hires the party to recover an illicit courier ledger from Merrit, a former bookkeeper claiming unpaid wages. But Merrit has tried to sell the book to a freight broker named Hollis. Hollis decided taking it would be cheaper. Now Merrit is barricaded inside an old parcel depot, and the supposed collection job has become an extraction.

The party can investigate, scout, break in, bluff their way through, negotiate, or fight. Inside, the rival crew is trying to force Merrit out and carry the ledger away through a lower loading passage. Recovering the book is only half the problem: they must get it out while protecting their source and deciding what to do with the man who stole it.

**DM secret:** the dispute, attempted sale, and rival crew are all Savone’s arrangement. Every planned participant belongs to his organization. The opposition genuinely tries to defeat the party’s plans within agreed limits; injuries and bad decisions can have real consequences. Savone wants capable associates who can adapt under pressure without exposing him.

**Status:** revised preparation, not played. The depot operation and revised NPC roles are proposals. The ledger’s origin, Savone’s caution, his affection for Po, and his loyalty to Faris carry forward. His precise rank and parity with the Gentleman remain undefined.

## At the table

- [[#1. Savone’s offer|Brief the party]]: book, authority, payment, privacy, possible resistance.
- [[#2. The missing bookkeeper|Discover the complication]]: Merrit has left the Kneaded Rest to meet a buyer.
- [[#3. The parcel depot|Present the site]]: three entrances, visible opposition, several routes to the objective.
- [[#4. A deal gone wrong|Find Merrit]]: the thief now needs extracting; he still has the book.
- [[#5. Recover and escape|Play the changing situation]]: rival crew, cargo lift, shutters, pursuit, or a negotiated exit.
- [[#6. Return and consequences|Resolve the job]]: return directly to Edda, then debrief Savone.

These are reference beats, not mandatory scenes. The party may skip the bakery, enter by any route, reach Merrit before meeting Hollis, or finish without combat. **Do not move an obstacle into their path just because they bypassed it.** Narration, dialogue, and private notes are labeled; DM assignments remain open.

## Grounding and cast

Sources: [[NPCs/Savone|Savone]], [[NPCs/Faris Harith|Faris]], [[The Moonhelm Society]], [[Sessions/Completed Events/Chein Po goes to the black market|Po’s introduction]], [[Sessions/Session Notes/008/Notes|008]], and [[Sessions/Session Notes/009/Notes|009]]. Session 009’s notes still mark it unplayed. The separate [[Sessions/Potential Events/Random/Family Heirloom|Family Heirloom]] lead is not part of this test.

Savone’s existing contact point is his carpet stall in the Bronze District. **All mission sites are separate from his carpet business.** The Kneaded Rest is a bakery with rented rooms; South Road Couriers uses a shared freight yard several streets away. An older parcel depot beside that yard is the main adventure site.

| Character | Apparent role and motivation | How to play them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Merrit** | Former bookkeeper; took the accounts over unpaid wages, then sought a buyer | Defensive until danger becomes immediate. Wants to leave alive and keep some bargaining power. |
| **Hollis** | Freight broker purchasing illicit client information, now attempting theft | Practical, patient, willing to fight for the book. Will trade when the cost of force grows. |
| **Rell** | Hollis’s muscle at the main entrance | Blocks access and holds ground; follows a clear order. |
| **Doss** | Hollis’s muscle at the records-room door | Works a pry bar, then protects the route to the loading passage. |
| **Nella** | Hollis’s lookout and runner | Watches the upper floor, relays what she sees, and carries the book out if she actually obtains it. |
| **Edda** | New owner of South Road Couriers | Off-site client and recipient; concerned with recovering her records and learning what promises were made. |

These reuse the mission’s existing aliases. Nella is no longer a dispatch-clerk conversation test, and Rell and Doss now work for the apparent rival. No extra contact chain is required.

## 1. Savone’s offer

**NARRATION**

> Savone waits until a browsing customer moves on. He closes his book and draws a folded set of papers from beneath the counter.

**SAVONE**

> “You asked about work. Something came across my desk that could use a few steady hands. A woman named Edda has bought South Road Couriers. Most of their parcels are ordinary enough. Some would interest the Watch rather more.”
>
> “Their old bookkeeper walked out with the accounts. Merrit. Says the previous owner Edda owes him wages. Edda disputes the amount and says it isn’t her debt. The last person she sent came back bruised and empty-handed. Now she’s talking about sending people who’ll do worse.”
>
> “I know people on both sides of this. I’d prefer to have the book back before someone does something expensive. Get it to Edda, keep the business private, and be reasonable with Merrit. He’s worth more alive than a debt settled over his body.”

**If asked why adventurers:** Merrit already turned away a lone messenger with help. Savone needs people who can handle resistance and an uncertain situation without immediately resorting to public force. Edda’s records include illicit deliveries; a complaint to the Watch would expose her own operation.

**SAVONE — terms**

> “A hundred and fifty for the group when she has it. Sixty more to negotiate with; bring back what you don’t spend. If you need to promise more, speak to me first. Keep my name out of it.”

**Give them:** Edda’s written authorization, a sale extract identifying the records, her description and freight-yard office address, Merrit’s address above the Kneaded Rest, and the expense purse. The book is blue cloth, with a split lower spine and South Road’s stamp inside the cover. Edda receives it directly; no intermediary is authorized to change that.

**PRIVATE:** The 150 gp fee and 60 gp advance remain draft amounts. Savone has not told them about a buyer. In the cover story, he does not know about the attempted sale. He provides no false guarantee that this is safe. Necessary inspection of the book is allowed; spreading its contents is not.

**If Po connects the offer to the Gentleman:**

> “I told you what I thought of that. This is work I can offer you. Decide whether you want it.”

## 2. The missing bookkeeper

Keep this short. It is an investigation scene, not another person to persuade.

**NARRATION**

> Warm bread and oven smoke follow you into the rear courtyard. Upstairs, Merrit’s door stands ajar. Clothes lie folded beside an open traveling bag. A cup has overturned across the desk; the wet edge of a letter curls against it. There is no blue book.

**Core clue — no roll:** the letter offers to buy the ledger and names the old parcel depot beside South Road’s freight yard, with a rough sketch of the entrance. It is signed Hollis. Merrit brought the book to that meeting. Searching the desk finds this; failure at a separate check cannot erase it.

**Useful extra observations:** a DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check distinguishes hurried packing from a violent search; the untouched purse and undisturbed latch support a voluntary departure. Failure leaves his reason uncertain, not the destination. A character who takes time to compare the physical evidence can reach the same conclusion without a roll.

**Other routes:** if the party visits Edda first, she gives the depot’s location and says Hollis has been asking about the accounts. She knows his interest through his inquiries, not mind-reading. Questioning bakery residents is optional; anyone who happened to see Merrit leave knows only that he went out carrying a wrapped book. Incidental residents are not Savone’s actors.

**PRIVATE:** The room is a rented staging site. Merrit left the letter because he was interrupted while packing by the agreed meeting time, not to leave a coded treasure hunt. Do not require theft from the bakery or harassment of its staff.

## 3. The parcel depot

The building once handled South Road’s overflow parcels; Merrit knows it from work. Hollis booked its disused sorting floor for a private purchase, away from the active yard. That explains both the meeting place and Merrit’s knowledge of a room he could barricade.

**NARRATION**

> The old depot sits beside the busy freight yard, its broad doors drawn almost shut. One man watches the narrow gap. Above him, a row of dusty windows follows a timber gallery. Around the side, a ramp drops below street level to a shuttered loading entrance. From somewhere inside comes the scrape of metal on wood, then a voice: “Put that down. Break in here and you’ll get nothing.”

**Visible facts:** Rell guards the main doors. Doss is levering a door inside, audible from outside. Nella’s movement is visible through the gallery windows to someone watching them. No check is needed for these basic facts or the three entrances. Hollis is inside negotiating with Merrit, not waiting to give a speech to the party.

### Site reference

The building footprint is roughly 60 by 40 feet. The gallery is 10 feet above the street-level sorting floor; the lower loading passage is 10 feet below that floor. The gallery runs along the walls, overlooking the sorting floor. Corridors and main stairs are 5 feet wide; the loading ramp is 10 feet wide.

| Area | Connections and contents | Useful choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Main doors / sorting floor** | Street entrance; stairs to gallery B and lower passage D; barricaded records room C at the back. Crate stacks interrupt sightlines. | Bluff or distract Rell; enter openly; hide between stacks; move a handcart to obstruct pursuit. |
| **B. Upper gallery** | Outside windows; stairs to A; hoist landing above D; maintenance hatch and fixed ladder into C. Nella begins here. | Climb in, scout through the railing, descend behind the barricade, or operate the hoist. |
| **C. Records room** | Barricaded door to A; ceiling hatch to B; narrow service door and short stair down to D. Merrit and ledger begin here. | Reach Merrit without breaking his door; negotiate through it; open another exit. |
| **D. Lower loading passage** | Stairs to A, service door to C, cargo platform to B, rolling shutter onto the exterior ramp. | Quiet exit, flank the crew, cut off a fleeing carrier, or isolate pursuers with the shutter. |

**Entrances and suggested checks:** these DCs are adventure proposals, not new house rules.

- **Main doors:** unbarred, watched by Rell. An adequate disguise or credible story may earn entry; use opposed Deception/Insight only when the lie is uncertain. Rell expects Hollis’s freight associates but has no secret password the party must guess.
- **Gallery window:** 15 feet above the courtyard; climbable masonry, an interior latch, and a narrow sill. DC 12 Athletics for an unaided hurried climb; a secured rope or suitable magic can remove that problem. DC 13 thieves’ tools to lift the latch quietly through the gap. Breaking the pane works and makes noise. Apply ordinary falling rules if a fall actually occurs.
- **Loading shutter:** locked from outside, operable from inside. DC 14 thieves’ tools or DC 16 Athletics to force the lock. Failure costs time or creates noise as appropriate; it does not summon another enemy. Picking it allows a quiet lower approach.

**Exploration:** checks reveal costs and opportunities; they do not replace planning. Use actual sightlines, noise, and the opposition’s senses for stealth. Flying, invisibility, disguises, familiars, and teleportation work according to their normal limits. Do not add wards because a spell bypasses part of the site.

### What happens if the party waits

At their first useful view of the site, Doss has begun prying the records-room door. Let them make an initial plan without burning an invisible timer. Then advance these stages after roughly ten minutes of substantial delay, announcing the audible or visible change. Checks alone do not advance time; sustained actions do.

1. **Pressure:** splintering wood; Hollis offers Merrit one last chance. This is the starting state.
2. **Breach:** the door gives way. Merrit retreats through C’s service door into D carrying the book; the crew follows. The party can intercept by any connected route.
3. **Removal:** if still unopposed, Hollis gains the book and the crew leaves through the loading ramp on foot with a handcart. Merrit is left bound in D. Follow the departing group or their visible cart tracks; failure to intervene indoors changes the adventure rather than ending it.

Once the party interferes, resolve events from positions and actions. Do not execute these stages over a fight or successful infiltration. No crew member can take the ledger without reaching its actual holder.

## 4. A deal gone wrong

**MERRIT — through the barricade**

> “If Hollis sent you, tell him I can still tear the names out. He’s buying a book, not me.”

Once the party identifies Edda, Merrit explains: he offered the ledger to Hollis for 120 gp; Hollis examined the cover, then ordered his people to take it. Merrit snatched it back and barricaded himself in the records room. This is an apparent escalation since Savone’s briefing, not a change to who originally owned the book.

**MERRIT — when he understands their job**

> “Fine. Edda gets her book. But you get me out with it. I’m not opening this door so you can leave me to them.”

**The book is real and stays put:** Merrit holds the identifiable blue ledger in a leather satchel. The entries are invented for the test; it is not a decoy swapped for a surprise second book. He has no hidden second objective.

**Play his changing priorities:** his opening claim remains 120 gp in wages, but safe escape now matters more. He accepts a credible extraction plan in exchange for the ledger; a payment up to the advance can settle his grievance too. Do not require payment and a roll and a rescue after the party makes a persuasive offer. He can pass the satchel through the ceiling hatch or open the service door when the group makes it safe.

Merrit can walk, climb with assistance, and follow simple directions. He is frightened, not deliberately incompetent. He will not run into an obvious attacker to create drama. He needs help because the crew controls the obvious exits, not because his legs stop working.

**Choice, not a moral trap:** taking the book and leaving Merrit is possible. Savone explicitly asked for restraint, but did not require martyrdom. An alternate safe escape for Merrit counts; withdrawing from an overwhelming fight counts as judgment. Assess what the party reasonably knew, not whether it performed a scripted rescue.

## 5. Recover and escape

**HOLLIS — if confronted**

> “He offered to sell it. Now he’s brought collectors into my meeting. I don’t particularly care who hired you. Leave the book and take him.”

Hollis wants the records and control of the exits. He does not know Savone hired the party in the public story; privately, he knows the test. He only learns their current approach through observation, noise, or a report from his crew.

### Opposition reacts

| Party action | Crew response | Opportunity for the party |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barges in or demands the book | Rell holds the entrance; Hollis comes into view to establish what they want. Doss stops prying only if called away or threatened. | Talk while another character scouts or reaches Merrit. |
| Reaches Merrit unseen | Crew continues at the barricade until it notices the escape. | Take an unguarded route; a clean extraction can avoid all combat. |
| Starts a fight on the sorting floor | Rell and Doss try to hold the stair approaches while Hollis moves toward the objective. | Use cover, divide the crew, reach C by B or D, or force Hollis away from the book. |
| Escapes toward the lower passage | Any observer calls out; a reachable pursuer heads for the shutter or follows. | Jam the shutter open, lower it behind the group, or leave by the gallery instead. |
| Hands the book to Hollis | He accepts it and orders the others to disengage. | Negotiate an authorized recovery, steal it back, or report an incomplete job honestly. |

**Nella’s task:** watch, report, then run the ledger out through D if Hollis actually hands it to her. She starts on B and needs time and movement to reach it. She does not acquire it off-screen. If she escapes, run pursuit using actual movement and sightlines. If she loses the party, their next lead is Hollis’s named freight desk at the active yard; his correspondence in the depot identifies it without a check. A second attempt remains possible.

### Let the building matter

- **Crates and handcarts:** provide cover where the actual stack blocks a shot. A character can push an unladen cart across a 5-foot aisle with an action, creating an obstacle that can be moved, climbed over, or bypassed. It does not magically restrain anyone.
- **Cargo hoist:** a secured platform connects D to B, bypassing the main stairs. One creature at the gallery winch uses an action to raise or lower it one level with up to two Medium riders. An unattended brake holds it in place. These are proposed equipment rules. Enemies can use it too. It has no automatic cable failure.
- **Rolling shutter:** an adjacent creature uses an action to lower or raise it from inside; securing its drop bar takes another action. Barred from the outside, it needs the lock forced or another route. Shout and describe the movement before someone closes it; no surprise crushing damage. A wedged cart prevents closure until moved.
- **Maintenance hatch:** offers a route between B and C. Opening it from above takes an action; a fixed ladder allows normal climbing. Enemies can follow if they discover it.

**A win can look like:** drawing the crew upstairs and slipping out below; covering Merrit while another character works the shutter; stealing the satchel while Hollis argues; overwhelming the crew and accepting surrender; persuading Hollis the records are worth less than the coming trouble; or negotiating a settlement with Edda’s authority. No single check makes every hostile cooperate without leverage.

**Negotiation under pressure:** Hollis can accept the 60 gp advance if confronted with a credible likelihood of defeat and an unobstructed departure. He can also retreat without payment if the party has clearly won. A claim to represent Edda can be verified from their papers. Savone’s name is never a necessary password. Allow the party to consult Edda or Savone; if they leave without securing the situation, use the announced clock.

**Why a fight starts:** the crew attacks when the party tries to force past it, carries the book through a controlled exit after refusing to stop, or attacks first. Hollis gives a clear demand before ordering force when he has the opportunity. Do not require the players to volunteer for a fight; opposition to their objective creates one naturally.

## Opposition and combat

**Roster for the confirmed five level-4 PCs:** Hollis uses the 2014 **Bandit Captain**, Rell and Doss each use **Thug**, and Nella uses **Spy**. Merrit uses **Commoner** and avoids combat. Use the full standard blocks from the [official 2014 Basic Rules](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/monsters); do not substitute their 2024 versions. These are encounter assignments for existing roles, not new custom NPC statistics.

**Difficulty check:** 450 + 100 + 100 + 200 = 850 raw XP; four contributing opponents give a ×2 multiplier, or **1,700 adjusted XP**. Five level-4 PCs have a Medium threshold of 1,250 and a Hard threshold of 1,875, so the full crew is an upper-Medium encounter by the [2014 encounter guidelines](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/basic-rules-2014/building-combat-encounters). This is a paper estimate, not a playtest. Extraction, divided positions, and remaining party resources change the practical challenge. The whole hostile roster is present from the start; no reinforcements appear just because the party is doing well. Separation and movement should matter, so do not begin all four surrounding one character.

**Tactics:** Hollis pressures the book’s carrier and directs withdrawals. Rell and Doss block routes, shove, grapple, and attack when needed; use the chosen stat blocks and normal action limits. Nella scouts and evades rather than standing in a damage line. Do not force her to flee with a book she does not hold. The crew is trying to recover property, not kill intruders.

**Morale:** Hollis offers a truce when two crew members are out of the fight or he falls below half his starting hit points. Individuals surrender or retreat when isolated and badly hurt. They stop if the party visibly surrenders. Retreating does not erase opportunity attacks or other normal consequences.

**Real danger inside a staged job:** the operatives use melee attacks and choose to knock a character unconscious when reducing them to 0 HP. This does not make all damage harmless: spells, falls, and player attacks remain real. They do not shoot down fleeing characters, attack unconscious people, or force a fight to the death. Defensive force by the party is an expected response, not an automatic moral failure.

**If the party loses:** the operatives stabilize fallen characters and abandon the exercise if necessary. Savone must account for what he arranged. Do not invent a prison adventure or confiscate unrelated equipment to extend the test.

## 6. Return and consequences

Edda receives them at her named South Road Couriers office in the active freight yard. They already have her address, description, and authority. Arrival goes directly to the handoff; Nella and Hollis do not reappear as reception staff.

**EDDA**

> “Is Merrit alive? And what did you have to promise to get this back?”

She checks the book and signs a receipt. She listens to any agreement about Merrit’s wages without retroactively authorizing extra expenses. She will not punish the party for opening the book to identify it or understand the danger. Public disclosure, copies sold to outsiders, or unauthorized transfer are separate matters.

**SAVONE — back at the stall**

> “How did it go?”

Let them tell it before asking questions. Savone settles the agreed fee and unused advance. His agents report separately afterward; he cannot quote events he has not yet heard about. If the book is lost, damaged, or held by someone else, resolve payment from the actual agreement and acknowledge honest reporting.

**Progress:** a successful test earns a later private conversation about what they want from the Gentleman and why. It does not expose Savone’s whole network. A mixed result narrows the next job or the information he will share. His loyalty to Faris and affection for Po do not disappear because her companions struggled.

## What Savone actually learns

| Question | Observable evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Can they investigate and adapt? | Following evidence, scouting the depot, changing plans when recovery becomes extraction. |
| Can they work as a group? | Coordinated entrances, covering movement, sharing information, avoiding abandonment through confusion. |
| Can they handle opposition? | Effective combat, infiltration, bargaining, or withdrawal. Avoiding a fight through skill is a success. |
| Can they protect a source? | What names and links they reveal under pressure. Silence or a polite refusal is valid. |
| Do they understand an objective? | Recovering the correct book, verifying authority, getting out rather than pursuing every enemy. |
| Can he trust their account? | How their report compares with witnessed actions, promises, receipts, and the recovered object. |

No hidden virtue score. Saving Merrit helps preserve a useful person; Savone also considers the actual risk. Defending themselves is expected. Executing surrendered opponents, exposing him unnecessarily, or knowingly lying about selling the book are distinct concerns. Agents report only what they personally saw or heard. Successful stealth can leave their assessment incomplete.

## If the test breaks

**What is staged:** the sale dispute, Hollis’s attempted theft, Merrit’s fear, the ledger entries, and every named participant’s public role. The organization has rented the rooms and depot. Actors know their starting positions, objectives, and abort instructions. They respond to events; they do not rehearse the party’s decisions. Incidental residents and the real Watch are not part of the arrangement.

**What Savone risks:** he has put his people and Po’s companions in a situation where real violence is plausible. He employs trained operatives and gives strict withdrawal orders, but cannot make that safe by declaration. If someone is seriously hurt, that is his responsibility as well as a consequence of the actions involved. Po may regard the entire exercise as a betrayal.

**Discoverable seams:** all ledger entries have suspiciously fresh ink; Hollis and Merrit use the same habitual hand signal when a dangerous mishap occurs; operatives prioritize stabilizing a fallen opponent over keeping the book. These invite investigation rather than proving the setup by themselves. Insight can reveal performance or withheld motives, not automatically download Savone’s plan.

**Abort:** any operative can call “The account is closed” if someone faces death, the real Watch arrives, or the deception is exposed. Others who hear stop attacking, offer aid, and say Savone arranged the job. This is an actual surrender and disclosure, not a magical pause. Unaware people keep acting until informed. Their ability to intervene follows their positions and normal rules.

Truth-seeking magic and recognition can expose the test. No blanket immunity protects the actors. If discovered early, allow the party to confront Savone; do not force them through the depot to finish a checklist.

**SAVONE — if confronted**

> “Yes. I arranged it. Faris trusts you, Po. I needed to know who I would be trusting with you.”
>
> “The names in that book are invented. The next people who might depend on you will not be. That was my reason. It doesn’t oblige you to like it.”
