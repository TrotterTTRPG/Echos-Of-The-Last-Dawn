## 1. Resume with the elder

The party already met him. Begin by asking for their recollection and retain any established words, promises, or impressions. Use the following staging only where it does not overwrite that meeting.

**Narration — proposed surroundings**

> Work continues around you. Shallow collecting trays stand beside the pond, and damp cloths hang above containers waiting for the last drops to fall. A worker scrapes a thin residue into a jar, studies how little it holds, and starts scraping again.
>
> Near the elder are containers set aside for dispatch. Some are almost empty. When a worker reaches toward one, he quietly moves it beyond their reach before turning back to you.

This makes extraction, scarcity, and the priority given to exports visible. Neither worker is a new named NPC. Do not prescribe a fight or a punishment for the attempted reach.

**Elder — opening**

> “You have met the woman upriver? Or you know something of her work?”

If they have not, he asks what brought them here. Let their answer establish what he knows. He is trying to identify useful outsiders, not administering a test of their goodness.

If they mention the job, the attacks, or the generator:

> “Then we are speaking about the same trouble. We collect what comes down the water. We send much of it home. There has not been enough, and there is less left to divide every day.”

**If asked what he needs most:**

> “The people waiting for our shipments must keep receiving them. If they stop, this little place will not be the end of the trouble.”

**If challenged about the workers going without:**

> “I know who is going without. I speak to them every day. But the people waiting at the other end do not become fewer because we are hungry here.”

He can mean this while prioritizing the trade. Do not require the party to accept his reasoning.

## 2. What he can tell them

Give concrete facts through conversation. Essential information does not require a successful social roll.

| Party asks | Elder's answer / performance |
| --- | --- |
| What are you collecting? | “We call it sugar. What we take from the pond.” He can show a small sample without demanding anyone consume it. |
| Why not gather cane yourselves? | “We did. It is sweet. It is not this.” They have tried cane and watched the operation from afar; he does not know the pond organism is the transformative step in this proposed presentation. |
| Why keep selling when you need it here? | “Because there are already people waiting for what we promised.” This establishes expectations, not a new prepaid contract or named debt. |
| Who are these people? | “Buyers. Back in the Umbral. They distribute it further.” Keep names, hierarchy, and particular threats loose. If pressed for an individual contact, establish one separately rather than inventing a larger court on the spot. |
| Is it addictive? | He acknowledges that people who do without are getting difficult to manage, including here. He may lack the party's vocabulary; he does not deny the observable dependency. |
| Did your fairies attack the factory? | “Some have gone there and attacked it. They would not wait.” The attacks are confirmed; whether he approved, tried to stop them, or knows their results remains a DM choice. Do not use this line as proof of innocence. |
| Why attack what supplies you? | “They know where it comes from. They want more of it. They have stopped listening to what happens after they get their hands on it.” This is his proposed explanation, not a verified account of every band's intentions. |
| What changed? | “She has been producing less. More people want what we send. We have used what we kept aside, and it still is not enough.” He knows the shortfall, not the precise reason for her reduced production. |
| Do you know what her machine does? | “I know what reaches us when she works it. I have watched the rest. I cannot tell you what every part is for.” |

**Reading him:** his attention keeps returning to the outgoing stock and unfinished work. Insight can reveal the priority or strain, not the identities of undefined buyers or knowledge he does not possess. Persuasion can change a negotiable term; it cannot refill the pond, cure dependence, or make buyers cease to exist.

## 3. The proposed bargain

> [!note] Confirmed opening offer; proposed terms
> The elder offers payment for the party to visit the Umbral buyers and negotiate more time. The DM has selected this opening. Messenger arrangements, wording, and compensation below are proposed implementation details. The party can negotiate or decline; a direct supply-restoration job is an alternative they may pursue.

**Elder — offer to visit the buyers**

> “They have heard my explanations. Hearing another will not improve them. Go with one of ours. Tell them you have come from this world, that you are looking into what has happened here. Ask them for time enough to do that.”

If the party has not agreed to investigate, stop and let them challenge that assumption. His proposed correction:

> “Then let us agree on what you are willing to do before I ask you to say it.”

He wants their presence to make his promise of action credible. He must not assume they understand the factory merely because they are material-world visitors. Let them correct him and decide what claim they are willing to make.

### Concrete terms to offer

**Proposed fee for this draft:** 300 gp for the party, 100 on agreement and 200 on returning with the buyers' answer. This assumes the elder can produce that amount; it is a proposed reward budget, not existing inventory or a payment already made. If retained, show the advance before departure. If a different reward is chosen, replace these numbers everywhere before play.

- **Task:** accompany the elder to the buyers, present the agreed truthful message, and help negotiate more time.
- **Success owed:** make the attempt and report accurately. The fee does not require the party to guarantee the buyers' cooperation.
- **No additional obligation:** they do not become guarantors, hostages, substitutes for sugar, or committed factory workers by accepting. A buyer demanding something further presents a new decision.
- **Travel:** the elder supplies the mushrooms and personally leads the crossing. He instructs everyone traveling to consume them at the same time so they can follow him, and advises against going alone. His own ability to cross back is established; the precise mechanism, destination selection, and the party's eventual return remain separate design questions.
- **Time requested:** the party and elder agree what amount of time they can honestly ask for. Do not invent an existing deadline.
- **Message:** write down the wording the party accepts. If he wants to overstate their role, give the party a chance to notice and object before anything is dispatched.

**If they want a supply job instead:**

> “Then tell me what you can find out here. I need something to send them besides another explanation.”

Offer to renegotiate the same proposed budget for an investigation and a practical report, with any restoration work agreed separately. He cannot promise the old woman's labor or authorize entry to her factory. That requires her agreement.

## 4. The mushrooms and an actual choice

The fairies call these mushrooms **Waycaps** (singular: **waycap**).

The mushrooms are offered after a bargain is discussed, not hidden in food. Hospitality can happen normally without determining who travels.

**Elder — explaining the crossing**

> “These are waycaps. They will take us over. Wait until everyone is ready. We must all take them together, so you can follow me.”
>
> “I would not advise making the journey alone.”

He keeps his own mushroom in hand as he explains. His tone is practical: this is how he expects to guide a group of unfamiliar travelers. His instruction is sincere travel advice, not a trick to prevent them observing a failure. The exact consequences of ignoring it have not been established.

**If asked where:**

> “To the Umbral. That is where the people you need to speak to are.”

**If asked whether others like them have done it:**

> “I have only known our people to use them. Why?”

He is sincerely puzzled by the concern, not smirking at a concealed danger. If they explain that their bodies or souls may differ:

> “I had not thought there was a difference that mattered. If there is, I cannot tell you what it would do.”

This allows his knowledge to change in response to the conversation. He does not continue claiming certainty after being given a specific reason to doubt. Nor does the narration disclose the purgatory mechanism before the party has learned it.

### Questions and preparation

- Let them examine the mushrooms, ask how fairies travel, and discuss returning. A fairy's successful use demonstrates fairy travel only, not safety for material creatures.
- Knowledge checks can reveal an appropriate limited observation or that the character has no reliable precedent. They do not need to fabricate a known recipe, saving throw, or guaranteed prognosis. Adjudicate spells and class features using the chosen rules and the later encounter design.
- Let them ask for a demonstration, stagger consumption, leave watchers, secure equipment, or decline. The elder recommends taking them together and following him, and discourages a solo attempt. The players still decide; do not call for a roll whose purpose is to make refusal turn into eating.
- Confirm each player's choice individually. A group agreement does not cause an unwilling PC, Kai, or Edgar to consume anything.
- The agreed group departure and the elder's approximately one-minute wait are scripted below. Other demonstration logistics, mushroom quantities, companion effects, and his return mechanism remain open; do not establish them accidentally in descriptive color.

### Branches at the table

| Choice | Continue with |
| --- | --- |
| All five agree | Confirm their message, bargain, and preparations; run the departure and the overheard call for help below before handing off to purgatory. |
| Only some agree | Preserve the division. Only consumers experience the established material-creature effect. Keep non-consumers active in Sugarbend; do not force them into purgatory for the two-table format. |
| One volunteers to try first | The elder advises traveling together with him. If the player insists, resolve the established partial-crossing effect for that consumer and let the others respond. Do not conceal their collapse or force later consumption to preserve the group scene. |

## 5. Taking the mushrooms — the crossing fails

**Confirmed sequence:** everyone who agrees takes the mushrooms together with the elder. His body and soul cross to the Umbral. The party fails to arrive. He waits about a minute, then travels back and finds their bodies on the ground, somewhat unconscious. Another fairy remains with the group during the attempted crossing. The party hears the elder recognize that something is wrong, then this other fairy calls for someone to fetch the village's doctor/herbalist.

This is prepared future play, not something that has already happened in session 008. The read-aloud below adds proposed sensory detail to the confirmed sequence. Adapt it to the actual consumers and any precautions they took; if someone chose to sit or lie down, do not narrate them falling from standing.

### Before they eat

**Elder:**
> “Ready? Together, then. Follow me.”

Let the players confirm. He eats his mushroom with them.

### The party's experience

**Narration:**
> The mushroom gives beneath your teeth. You swallow.
>
> The elder is there in front of you—and then the space where he stood is empty.
>
> You are still here.
>
> The ground comes closer. You feel yourself settle against it, but the sensation is distant, as though it is happening somewhere you cannot quite reach. Voices continue around you. You can hear the sounds, but keeping hold of the words takes more effort than it should.
>
> You cannot tell how long you have been lying there.

Pause. Do not narrate the elder's view of the Umbral to the players. For the DM, he has waited there approximately one minute for them to join him before returning. This interval is not the total duration of the party's purgatory experience and establishes no general time ratio between planes.

**Narration:**
> A voice comes into focus above you. The elder's.
>
> “No. Something is wrong. They should have come through.”
>
> A different voice cuts in—another fairy who has been beside you.
>
> “Go and get the herbalist. Quickly—all of them are still here.”
>
> Footsteps hurry away. Someone is speaking close beside you now, but the words are becoming difficult to hold onto again.

Use “doctor” instead of “herbalist” if that is the village's eventual title; this is one still-undefined medical role, not two new named NPCs. Adjust “all of them” if only some PCs took mushrooms. The elder's concern is genuine; the other fairy takes the initiative to call for help. This is the final material-world beat before the later purgatory encounter begins; do not add its opening room or resolution here.

### What the elder and any watchers see

He genuinely reached the Umbral, did not find the party following, and came back. Their bodies remain on the ground in a partly unconscious state. Any non-consuming PC witnesses the failure and can act while he is away; the scene does not require everyone present to wait passively for his return.

The fairy who stayed with the group calls for the village doctor/herbalist on seeing that something is wrong. The elder does not give this order, and neither fairy knows that the party's souls are in purgatory. The doctor/herbalist arranges their care and transfer to Sugarbend's version of a clinic. Both elder and doctor assume accidental poisoning. The doctor uses healing medicines and magic to stabilize the party. Their identity, arrival time, and exact preparations or spells remain open. Do not promise a cure or decide those details through this call for help. The elder's precise means of returning is also not specified here; do not invent an extra dose or universal return rule.

Proposed response after the call: make space around the bodies, prevent accidental injury, and cooperate with any companions who remained. If Tansy is present, she can help with practical care. No further mushrooms are automatically administered. The bodies are subsequently moved to the village clinic, as established in the awakening scene below. Healing medicines and magic are used to stabilize them; exact spells, preparations, HP effects, and the party's bodily condition during the full absence remain to be defined. “Somewhat unconscious” here is the DM's narrative description, not an assigned HP total, death-saving-throw state, or new condition rule.

### Handoff and scope

The material-plane consumers' souls have detached from their bodies and entered purgatory instead of the Umbral. If all five participate, use the approved split: Aulberos/Vivatrix Umbral aspect; Aelar/Chein Effulgent aspect; Naieht ghostlike in both, meaningfully interacting with neither. Partial participation does not fill empty places with unwilling characters.

**Still omitted:** purgatory descriptions, rooms, puzzles, communication mechanics, hazards, and the means of returning to their bodies. No six-hour duration or automatic long rest carries over from v1.

For characters staying behind, retain their choices: assist with care, question the elder, examine the supply problem, send a message, or speak to the old woman. Coordinate elapsed time with the later purgatory encounter. Do not invent simultaneous attacks merely to give them a combat.

## 6. Coming to in Sugarbend's clinic

**Confirmed staging and beliefs:** the party's bodies have been moved to Sugarbend's version of a clinic. The doctor has been using healing medicines and magic to stabilize them. **The elder and doctor do not know the party traveled at all. They assume the elder accidentally poisoned them.** The doctor criticizes his decision while showing little personal concern about whether the patients ultimately live or die.

Their souls' actual return belongs to the later purgatory encounter. Stabilizing their bodies does not establish that the doctor recalled their souls. Exact medicines, spells, HP effects, and duration remain open; do not grant a full heal or long rest merely because care occurred. Their mission to the Umbral buyers has not happened off-screen.

The clinic staging and dialogue below are proposed delivery of these established beats. Adapt them to any companions who remained, actual care arrangements, and the sequence of awakenings.

### The space

Use the working village's treatment shelter: drying herbs, cloth, basins, small work surfaces, and equipment brought in to accommodate larger patients. Padded collecting frames serve as improvised beds. Their packs and belongings remain beside them unless actual play established another arrangement.

Proposed transfer: the doctor directed villagers to carry the bodies here. Remaining PCs could supervise or help; do not overwrite their actions. Evidence of ongoing treatment includes used cloths, opened medicine pots, and the doctor moving between patients, applying healing magic as needed. These details establish care without selecting a named spell or inventing consumed party resources.

### Read aloud — waking into the argument

> Something rough brushes your cheek. Woven cloth. Beneath it, a firm edge presses against your shoulder.
>
> The air smells of crushed leaves and damp wood. You hear something being ground in a bowl, then a sharp tap as a tool is set down.
>
> “And you had seen them eat these before?”
>
> The elder answers, close by. “No. I have only seen our people use them.”
>
> “Yes. I gathered that.”
>
> You open your eyes to a low roof of woven reeds. Bundles of herbs hang from the supports. The elder stands beside a small worktable while another fairy wipes their hands on a cloth.
>
> “We use them all the time,” the elder says.
>
> “We do. They don't.”
>
> “Will they live?”
>
> The other fairy looks along the improvised beds. “They're doing better. If they die, you'll need different messengers. Hold this.”
>
> They put a bowl into the elder's hands, then notice you watching.
>
> “Oh. That one's awake.”

Pause for the player to respond. Their first words can interrupt the conversation earlier. This is ordinary bedside dialogue, not a cutscene the players must silently endure. Other characters wake as their actual returns occur; do not assume the whole group opens their eyes together.

### The doctor at the bedside

**Doctor:**
> “You're in the clinic. He gave you something that disagreed with you. I've been keeping you alive while it worked through.”

That last explanation is the doctor's belief. It does not establish how the mushrooms wear off or how the souls returned.

**Playing the doctor:** competent, occupied, and unsentimental. They have actually spent medicines and magic on treatment. Their lack of emotional investment is not concealed murderous intent, and does not mean they withheld all care. The elder's careless assumption and the work it created bother them more than the possibility of these strangers dying. Deliver the criticism in an ordinary working tone; no theatrical cruelty is needed.

They address the patient with brief practical questions: “Can you hear me clearly?” “Can you sit?” They can offer water or assistance, leaving acceptance to the player. Do not impose new weakness, injury, or helplessness solely through narration.

### Questions the party may ask

| Question | Proposed response / handling |
| --- | --- |
| What happened? | Doctor: “You ate his waycaps and collapsed. He brought me a roomful of poisoned visitors.” This is their diagnosis, not the DM truth. |
| What did you do to us? | “Healing medicines. Healing magic. Enough to keep you going. You were already in this state when I arrived.” Name specific treatments only when selected; do not invent a dose recipe, spell list, or mechanical cure. |
| Did we die? | Describe only established observations. Stabilization does not automatically mean zero HP, death saves, or resurrection. The doctor knows nothing of detached souls. |
| How long were we out? | Give the elapsed time established with the eventual encounter. Do not import v1's six hours. |
| Did you bring us back? | Doctor: “I kept you alive. You've finally woken up.” Neither doctor nor elder interprets “back” as a return from another plane unless the party explains it. |
| Where are our belongings and companions? | Give their actual locations. No unplayed theft, confiscation, or companion disappearance. |
| Does that mean you would have let us die? | Doctor: “I've been treating you. That doesn't mean I can promise you an outcome.” They are not devastated by the possibility of failure, but they have been working. |

If the party describes purgatory, the doctor's initial interpretation is an experience caused by the poisoning:

> “You were out of your senses. I wouldn't expect it to have felt ordinary.”

This is a fallible interpretation. Let the party compare accounts, present evidence, or decline to explain. Neither NPC gains knowledge of purgatory, the soul aspects, or Vivatrix's patron merely by hearing that the group had an unusual experience. Neither must remain impervious to convincing evidence in later play.

### The elder's response

He is present for the argument in this proposed staging. He believes his attempted guided journey poisoned the visitors and failed completely; **he does not ask where they traveled**.

**Elder:**
> “I crossed over. I waited, and none of you came. When I came back, you were on the ground. I thought the waycaps would work for you as they do for us.”

**Doctor, without looking up:**
> “You thought.”

**Elder, to the party:**
> “Yes. I was wrong.”

Let the players respond. His explanation can be sincere without obliging them to forgive him. His original confidence and present belief in poisoning are consistent: he has encountered an outcome he did not expect and adopted the doctor's explanation.

**If they say they actually traveled:**
> “You went somewhere? But you were here when I returned.”

Treat this as new information he is trying to understand, not recognition that he secretly knew where their souls had gone. His minute in the Umbral did not include an off-screen buyer meeting or successful negotiation.

### Return to the unfinished situation

Village work continues outside. One optional detail—an argument over a ration or a worker waiting for the elder—can remind the party that the existing scarcity has not vanished. Do not invent another raid or buyer punishment during their absence without a separate decision.

After the party has had room to react, the elder can say:

> “The people waiting for our shipments are still waiting. But you did not agree to this. We should settle what I promised you.”

Resolve payment and the party's willingness to remain involved. Medical care, belongings, and permission to leave are not bargaining chips in this draft. The doctor's indifference does not itself create a medical debt, threat, or hostage arrangement.

### If the party is angry

> “I told you what I believed. It was not enough. You agreed to a journey, and I did not get you there.”

Proposed ruling for the drafted bargain: the advance stays with them. He offers the remaining 200 gp as settlement for the attempted journey and failure of his promised transport, rather than claiming they owe him a successful second attempt. This is a negotiation position, not a rule forcing the party to forgive him. Any further job needs a new agreement and an explicitly available reward.

He still wants a solution and may ask for one, but does not make payment conditional on silence, taking another dose, or pretending they reached the buyers.

### If they want to help despite the failure

> “Then let us do what can be done here. Tell me what you need to learn, and what you need me to stop doing while you learn it.”

The material-world routes below are available whether the party traveled, refused, or split. The trip is not a prerequisite for understanding the pond.

## 7. Find what the supply actually depends on

The causal chain is established: **cane juice → sugary runoff → pond organism's transformation → extraction and distribution**. The fairies know raw cane is different; their complete understanding of the biological step is not established. For this proposed investigation, the elder has not identified that step. The old woman also begins unaware of the addictive downstream product.

### Evidence that can be pursued

| Location / action | Proposed obtainable evidence | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare raw cane, fresh outflow, and pond product | The distinctive product appears after passage through the pond, not merely after crushing cane. Supply samples through observation and assistance; do not require ingestion. | Exact organism, cure, instantaneous mass production, or moral innocence. |
| Inspect collection surfaces and shallow pond margins | A recurring film/growth is associated with productive areas. Keep its species unnamed until chosen. | That scraping it all out is safe, or that every growth in the pond is useful. |
| Watch the workers and ask about yields | Collection has fallen while requests have increased; the reserves used to cover the gap are depleted. | Invented shipment counts, a complete cutoff, or a claim that attacks caused the initial decline. |
| Observe the factory with permission | Where cane enters, what water leaves, and what the woman actually changes or controls. | Whether her explanation about souls is correct or incorrect. |
| Conduct a small supervised comparison | Samples exposed to the pond growth produce a different result from untreated samples after an appropriate observation period. | A selected brewing time or guaranteed successful scale-up. Those depend on the eventual process details. |

Use relevant Nature, Investigation, tools, and character abilities to improve specificity and confidence. Let a sensible multi-step comparison establish the basic connection without gating the entire solution behind a single roll. One check should not furnish a complete production recipe.

The Umbral/Effulgent properties of purgatory have no role in this reaction. The travel mushrooms are not automatically the pond organism or a treatment for sugar dependence.

## 8. Speak with the old woman

The party needs a way to approach without looking like another attack. Ask how they announce themselves and whether they arrive with fairy representatives, drawn weapons, or evidence. Prior violence matters, but its unspecified outcomes must not become invented deaths or ruins.

**Old woman — if they come to talk**

> “If you are here about the fairies, say so before you come any closer to the works.”

**If they say Sugarbend needs her operation:**

> “Then someone ought to tell the ones who keep attacking it.”

**If shown the pond product and told where it comes from:**

> “That comes out of the water I let go? Show me what you mean.”

She is intelligent and willing to examine a concrete observation. Whether her soul theory is correct remains unresolved. She can describe the machinery in terms of moving souls while accurately pointing out valves, loads, and work steps.

### Proposed negotiating positions

| Person | Wants | Can reasonably offer | Cannot promise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elder | Enough product for buyers; a viable collection workforce | Worker cooperation, information, carrying a message, and an attempt to restrain bands he can influence | Instant compliance from all addicted fairies or unnamed Umbral buyers |
| Old woman | Her operation and daughters safe; work able to continue | Access for a supervised investigation, an agreed handling of outflow, or a trial change within the actual limits of her equipment and labor | Infinite output, a cure, or responsibility for every buyer's demand |
| Party | Whatever they negotiate or value | Mediation, investigation, practical work, protection they actually undertake | Control of either side simply because they rolled well |

Let Ally and Mary participate where their presence is established; do not assign them unapproved injuries, betrayal, or specialist expertise. The stolen Dornthalm diagrams can remain a separate discovery for Aelar. Do not make exposing that theft mandatory to solve the pond problem.

**A practical provisional agreement:** the elder communicates a halt to further attacks and sends workers willing to cooperate; the woman permits a supervised inspection and agrees on outflow handling consistent with what the investigation establishes; the party reports what is supported by evidence. If the elder cannot control a band, he admits it. Any security or additional work is separately agreed, not silently added to the original fee.

## 9. Resolve the two pressures together

**Confirmed situation:** the electric woman has been producing less recently, while the number of sugar addicts continues to grow. The depleted reserves can no longer cover the difference. A recovery plan must address both the input and the commitments made against it.

### Proposed reason her output fell: the air pump is failing

> [!note] New implementation proposal, not yet a settled fact
> The apparatus already contains an air pump. This draft proposes a leaking pump seal that forces her to run the operation less often. Use this as a concrete, repairable problem if approved; otherwise replace this subsection with the chosen reason for reduced production. Keep the confirmed reduced-output/growing-demand situation in either case.

She can still operate the factory, but the pump loses pressure and must be stopped and attended to repeatedly. Running less means processing less cane and sending less sugary water downstream. This is not evidence that she intentionally cut Sugarbend off, that she understands the pond transformation, or that the earlier attacks originally caused the fault.

**Observable evidence for this proposal:** a persistent hiss at the pump, difficulty lifting the weighted stone, and the woman stopping work to inspect the same fitting. If production is halted when they arrive, she can demonstrate the trouble under her control. No need to invent a diagnostic instrument or make her incapable of finding an obvious leak; she knows the failing part, but has not achieved a lasting repair.

**Old woman:**
> “It will lift the stone. Then it starts losing its breath, and I spend the rest of the day putting it right again. I cannot keep crushing cane while I am keeping this thing together.”

**Proposed repair terms:** a replacement seal and a careful fitting/test, using competent help. A working estimate is half a day and 25 gp in materials, separate from any party fee; these are new preparation values, not costs already incurred. Establish who supplies the material and pays before work begins. Relevant tools, suitable spells, a brought-in craftsperson, or a different sound repair plan can solve it. Do not force one skill check or forbid a valid ability solely to preserve this estimate.

A successful test restores her previous ordinary throughput. It does not make production unlimited. If the party cannot repair it immediately, arranging a part or assistance and setting an honest interim schedule is a valid negotiated result. The old woman can retain control of her machinery.

### Establish what the pond does

The comparative investigation in section 7 can show that the special product emerges in the pond. Restoring the factory's input and identifying the biological step can be pursued separately; do not require the party to solve all of the alchemy before repairing a known pump fault.

The finding gives Sugarbend a longer-term possibility: learn to manage the organism and its feed more deliberately. That is a future trial, not an instant replacement for the factory or permission to declare a limitless supply. More sugar introduced blindly is not an automatically successful experiment.

### Negotiate the allocation and backlog

The elder has to acknowledge that even restored production cannot satisfy an indefinitely growing market. Ask for a concrete commitment, rather than narrating that everyone agrees:

- What can Sugarbend reliably collect at the restored rate? Use observation to establish an estimate; do not invent precise historic volumes.
- What portion goes to the buyers, and what minimum stays with the workers? His buyer-first priority makes this a real negotiation. He is not automatically persuaded to prioritize local consumption instead.
- Which promises need to be reduced or delayed? Another fairy messenger can carry an honest report even though the party's attempted journey failed.
- Who communicates with the woman and her daughters, and what can the elder actually do to discourage further bands from attacking?
- When will they review whether the agreed arrangement is working? Pick a credible interval once production and transformation time are established.

**Elder — when pressed to promise more than the recovered operation can provide:**
> “You want me to send them an amount I know they will find insufficient.”

Let the party answer. If persuaded to state the limits honestly, he does so because the alternative is less credible, not because his attachment to the buyers vanishes.

### What counts as resolution here

A workable material-world resolution has three parts: a practical way to restore or sustain the woman's output, a collection/allocation plan that admits the growing demand, and an agreed message to the Umbral buyers. Record which parts actually happened.

The immediate dispute can end with cooperation and a recovery plan even while the buyer response remains a future thread. No off-screen acceptance is assumed. Restored runoff takes time to become extracted product; it does not instantly refill reserves or cure dependency. The party may accept those limits, negotiate further, choose a different approach, or leave.
