## Room 1 — The Threshold

> You're standing at the start of a garden path that shouldn't exist — the hedges on either side are dead, brittle, black-branched things trimmed at some point into shapes you can almost recognize before your eye slides off them. The air smells like wet ash and something sweet underneath it, gone slightly off, like fruit a day past ripe. Somewhere off to your left, something is making a sound like a bird might, if it had learned birdsong secondhand and never quite gotten it right.
>
> Blocking the path is a gate. Wrought iron, corroded to a deep rust-black, the vines grown through it long dead and brittle, crumbling to dust wherever they're touched. In the center of the gate, four glyphs are set in a ring, each rusted into place. Beneath them, scratched into a corroded plaque, a few lines of old verse, half the letters eaten through:
>
> *"... ... then, the warmth that feeds the need... ... ... last, the dark where lost things stay."*

**The ring (clockwise from the top, as they face the gate):**
- **Top** — a full sun-disc, straight rays all the way round, like a child's drawing of the sun.
- **Right** — a **solid black disc**, no rays, no marks. Just dark.
- **Bottom** — a **half-sun** sitting on a horizontal line, short rays fanning up.
- **Left** — another **half-sun**, sinking *below* a horizontal line, rays fanning up. Pitted enough that it looks almost identical to the one at the bottom.

**What this table can work out:** the full sun is *"the warmth that feeds the need"* (Noon) and the black disc is *"the dark where lost things stay"* (Midnight — and the verse says *last*). The two half-suns look nearly identical; **they cannot tell which is Dawn and which is Dusk without asking the other table which positions their bud and their closing bloom are in.** The missing lines (*"First, the light that wakes the seed"* and *"then, the hush that dims the day"*) are legible on the Effulgent side.

**The full solution:** **Bottom → Top → Left → Right** (half-sun on the line, full sun, half-sun sinking, black disc). Touching each in order makes it grind and settle. **A wrong touch makes all four rust back into place** with a dull *clunk* the other table can hear. **The gate opens only when both tables have finished** — the rust crumbles at a touch and the whole thing collapses inward into a drift of orange flakes, gone, no fanfare.

*Texture to drop in if they linger:* something is dripping steadily nearby, though there's no visible water; the dead hedge-shapes seem to have turned, fractionally, to face wherever the party's standing, though nothing was seen to move; a single perfect apple sits in the dead grass, unrotted, gleaming — the only clean thing in the whole scene. It means nothing. Let them worry about it for nothing.

**[VIV] — deliver this quietly, to Vivatrix alone, once, whenever feels natural in this room:**

> *The others are wrinkling their noses at the wet-ash smell. You're not. If anything, you're breathing it in a little deeper than you meant to — it's not pleasant, exactly, but it doesn't sit wrong in your chest the way it clearly does in theirs. It smells like somewhere familiar. You couldn't say where.*

## Room 2 — The Tapestry and the Door

> The path opens into a round chamber, its honey-colored stone gone soot-dark and cold, open to a starless black sky through a gap in the ceiling far overhead. On the far wall hangs a door — heavy, warped, half off its lower hinge, hanging at a wrong angle. Old scorch marks fan out from the frame. It looks like it's been like this for a very long time, and like it's still, somehow, the only way through.

**This table has the way forward.** The door isn't locked; it's wedged and warped. A solid shove or a plain Athletics check (DC 10) opens it. Failing only makes noise. **The other table can't open anything without this table's help,** and they may not know it yet — encourage them to talk through the wall.

**When this table forces the door** (signal your co-DM immediately):

> The door gives all at once, shrieking off its last hinge and slumping sideways into the dark beyond — and past it, where you'd expect only more ruin, there's a passage, plain and unremarkable, like it was cut there on purpose and simply waited for someone to notice. Somewhere in the black beyond the doorway, you'd swear something is still swaying, gently, the way a hanged thing sways in a draft. It's nothing. There's nothing there when you look closer.

**If the other table pulls something down first** (a heavy fall of cloth): the door judders in its frame, but doesn't open on its own. It needs someone on *this* side.

*Texture:* the soot-dark walls are cold enough to raise gooseflesh, colder than the room's air should make them; a faint, tuneless hum seems to come from inside the door's wood grain itself, never resolving into anything; if anyone examines the door's scorch marks closely, they form, very nearly, the shape of a hand pressed flat against it from the other side. This means nothing. Let them chase it.

## Room 5 — The Shrine of Two Mothers

> Past the star-woven crossing, the path narrows into a hushed, ash-grey grove centered on a cracked stone altar. Atop it, a statue: a tall, gaunt matron, robes carved in folds gone brittle-looking with age, arms outstretched — not welcoming so much as *reaching.* A pair of ravens perch in the crook of one carved arm, silent, watching the party with too much attention for birds. A shallow dish at her feet holds a thin curl of ash instead of incense, still faintly warm, as if something burned there recently and finished only just before the party arrived.
>
> There's no instruction carved anywhere. Just the statue, waiting, arms open, the way you'd wait for someone to come home — or the way you'd wait for someone to finally stop running.

**[VIV] — deliver this quietly, to Vivatrix alone, the first time she focuses on the statue or the ash:**

> *The ravens should unnerve you. They don't. If anything, the way they've settled — watchful, patient, utterly unbothered by the gaunt reaching thing they're perched on — feels less like a warning and more like an invitation. The ash smells clean to you. Not sweet. Just* right*, the way a room smells right after you've lived in it long enough to stop noticing.*

**If anyone examines the base of the statue, worn words are still legible:**

> *"Bring what you would be glad to lose. She keeps nothing that is not given up gladly."*

It's not a riddle. If a player asks what it means, that's exactly what it means: something they're releasing on purpose — a grudge spoken and let go, a drop of blood, a confession — not something taken from them.

**The puzzle:** the statue wants an offering given **up** — an old grudge spoken aloud and released, a drop of blood, a treasured but non-magical item left behind, a genuine confession. Let each player choose their own; don't prescribe one. Once everyone's ready, coordinate a countdown across the wall with the Effulgent table ("on three") — **both rooms must offer at the same moment.**

**If a player asks who she is:** nobody answers. If anyone gets too hung up on it, the ravens shift, the ash stirs, and the moment moves on. She's simply the **Mother of Both Halves** and the dream doesn't explain her.

**When both offerings land simultaneously:**

> The ravens lift off together, circle once, and vanish into the ash-grey canopy overhead. The statue's stone hands close, gently, as if finally letting go of something it had been holding far too long — and the grey in the grove **warms.** It doesn't lift so much as *soften,* deepening into the color of lantern-glow, and the smell of ash thickens until it's the smell of spiced wine and woodsmoke. You feel very, very tired, in the way you feel at the end of a long, hard evening.

Then cue the wake-up (see below).

*Texture:* the ash in the offering dish, if watched closely, seems to briefly hold shapes — a wing, a face, a door — before scattering; the altar stone is worn smooth in the exact shape of countless hands that came before, in a spot none of the party has touched yet.

> The grey behind your eyelids warms to the orange of a lantern, and you're sitting at the feast table. Your cup is where you left it. But the sky above the trees has gone deep, star-scattered black, and the air has the cold, damp stillness of the small hours. The village is *quiet.* Your mouth tastes like sugar and old smoke.

**[VIV] — deliver this quietly, to Vivatrix alone, before the rest of the scene starts:**

> *You wake more rested than you've felt in weeks. The others are groggy, rubbing their eyes, slow to find their feet. You aren't. When you open your hands, there's a faint smudge of grey ash on your palms that wasn't there when you sat down. You don't wipe it off right away.*
