## Start here

**The truth:** Waycaps normally carry fairy bodies and souls into the Umbral. Oren Reedwick, Sugarbend’s elder, sincerely expects to lead the party there to bargain for more time with his buyers. For these material-plane travelers the mushrooms instead detach souls into **one shared purgatory with two aspects**. Bodies remain in Sugarbend. This is an accident, not a deliberate prison or test devised by Oren.

**The aspects:** Effulgent approaches perfect order, completeness, abundance, and stillness. Umbral approaches change, entropy, decay, and emergence. Neither is inherently good or evil. These are near states, not the pure planes. The environment may respond to visitors; do not describe the Effulgent surroundings as constantly bustling or the Umbral as eternally motionless ruins.

Companion travel effects remain undefined; do not feed companions mushrooms by narration.

**Practical defaults for this draft:**

- Keep players at one table, label their aspects, and use one shared diagram. Their souls can hear one another across aspects here. They perceive the same layout through different appearances. This communication is an encounter rule, not a universal planar ability.
- Characters move, talk, and handle the scene’s objects normally within their aspect. Object changes specified by the puzzle appear in both. No special messenger is required.
- Alternate attention between aspects after a meaningful action. Ask a different player to operate, inspect, or lead at each new step; do not force a silent player or monopolize their character.
- Puzzle actions require no spell slots and no guessed philosophy. No mandatory saving throw, combat, or damage has been added. An unsuccessful puzzle action gives visible feedback and can be corrected. This is not a promise that all possible actions in purgatory are harmless.
- Track any resources the players actually spend. Do not award a rest, replenished slots, automatic healing, permanent powers, or a real item from a manifested prop.
- The garden opens a route that reunites souls and bodies. Medical care stabilizes bodies but is not secretly the puzzle’s solution. This is the draft’s proposed return mechanism.
- Total material-world absence has no established value. Record intervening clinic events rather than assuming six hours. If a precise time is needed, select it before the departure and note it below; do not derive it from Oren’s one-minute wait.

**Physical materials:** paper, five character tokens, three bridge strips, three plant-bed markers, and five stage labels: Seed / Shoot / Bloom / Fruit / Soil. Tokens can be coins. No hidden handouts, app, or separate room is necessary.

## 3. The crystal arch

### Understand the whole puzzle first — DM only

**Think of a doorway with two broken doorposts and a solid block wedged into the opening.** The block prevents anyone walking through, but it also props up the arch above. If the party removes the block before fixing both posts, the arch sinks and the magical block grows back. They must make the side supports reliable before removing the thing in their way.

There is **one physical arch**, seen differently by the two groups. An action on one side changes the same structure the other side sees. The characters can hear each other and coordinate.

**The four parts:**

| Part | What it does at the start | What the party needs to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left pillar | A cracked support; cannot reliably carry its side of the arch | An Effulgent character holds it steady with a hand. |
| Right pillar | The same problem on the other side, 10 feet away | A second Effulgent character holds this one steady. |
| Central slab / growth / blockage | These names all mean **the same object**: the crystal block filling the doorway and helping support its top | An Umbral character touches it and lets it crumble away. |
| Curved arch overhead | Rests on the pillars and the central block | Nobody needs to operate it. Its movement shows whether the pillars are supported. |

**Exactly how the magic works:** Effulgent touch makes the touched crystal hold its shape. Umbral touch makes it loosen and break apart. These effects are automatic here: no spell, skill check, special item, or spoken command is required. A tentative touch shows the effect; keeping a hand there continues it. A person does not need to know the solution before experimenting.

**The complete solution, step by step:**

1. **Aelar places a hand on the left pillar and keeps it there.** That pillar’s cracks align; it becomes stable.
2. **Chein places a hand on the right pillar and keeps it there.** That pillar also becomes stable. Either Effulgent character may take either side; Naieht may substitute if assigned Effulgent.
3. **While both pillars are being held, Aulberos or Vivatrix touches the central block and loosens it.** It crumbles away. Either Umbral character can do this; Naieht may substitute if assigned Umbral.
4. **The arch now stands on its two stable pillars, with an open passage beneath it.** Completing this combination makes the stability persist. The two holders can remove their hands and everyone can walk through. No one has to stay behind.

The first two steps can happen in either order. They do not need to time three touches to the same instant; **both pillar holds must simply overlap while the center is removed**. The whole party can work from the courtyard side. They do not need to reach through the block or cross first.

**What happens if they try something else:**

- **Remove the center with neither pillar held:** both sides of the arch sag, and the center regrows. Back to the starting state; no automatic damage.
- **Remove the center with only one pillar held:** only the unsupported side sags, and the center regrows. This visibly identifies the missing step. The held pillar stays stable while contact continues.
- **Let go of a pillar before the center is successfully removed:** that pillar becomes unstable again. They can put a hand back and retry.
- **Use Effulgent touch on the center:** it becomes more solid. They have strengthened the blockage, not opened the passage. Letting go undoes this.
- **Use Umbral touch on a pillar:** it crumbles, and the arch settles against the central block. When the character stops, the pillar regathers into its original unstable state. They can retry.

**Why this puzzle exists:** it teaches the players the two aspects’ touch effects through a simple shared task. They are stabilizing useful supports and removing an obstruction. There is no riddle, correct symbol count, hidden order, or required speech about balance.

**How to run discovery:** describe the cracked pillars and separate central block. Ask what they touch or inspect. Show the appropriate reaction immediately, including what the other group sees. If they try removing the center first, emphasize that the unsupported pillars gave way. Let that observation lead them to try holding the sides.

**Winning condition:** both pillars are stable when the central block is removed. Announce a clear open passage and confirm that it stays open after the holders let go. The detailed descriptions below provide narration for each experiment; they add no further requirements.

**A limitation to know upfront:** this is a freestanding arch, and the current scenario allows a sensible route around or over it. It is a teaching opportunity, not a compulsory sealed gate. If the party bypasses it, let that work and demonstrate the touch effects on accessible crystal before the bridge; do not invent an invisible wall.

**Initial state to track:** left pillar unstable; right pillar unstable; center present. That is the entire puzzle state until success.

### Approaching the arch

**DM NOW:** Deliver the arrival description for each aspect, then pause. The inspection passages below are responses to what players examine, not a speech to read all at once. Every visible clue is available without a check. Their appearances differ, but both groups are examining the same two supports, curved arch, and central slab.

**READ ALOUD — Effulgent**

>

>

**READ ALOUD — Umbral**

### What closer examination reveals

**Looking at either pillar — Effulgent**

> The crack runs around the pillar, not just across its face. Above and below it, the crystal’s branching lines almost meet. Almost. One is displaced by the width of a hair. Once you notice it, the tiny mismatch stands out against everything around it.

**Looking at either pillar — Umbral**

> The crack opens and narrows under the weight above it. Within it, a ridge on one section catches against a hollow in the other, slips, then catches again. The pillar is still standing, but its pieces are not holding their places.

**DM clue:** both pillars have the same structural problem. If players examine the second after the first, confirm that plainly. The branch patterns are visual evidence of misalignment, not a symbol-counting puzzle.

**Examining the central slab — Effulgent**

> A seam separates the slab from each pillar. Its lower edge meets the paving without a gap, and its top presses firmly beneath the arch. It has no handle, hinges, or keyhole. Along its face, even the smallest imperfections end in a complete crystal shape.

**Examining the central slab — Umbral**

> A loose cluster near the surface flakes away under the lightest pressure. For an instant there is a shallow hollow. New points rise from its bottom and close it again. The surface is constantly breaking and rebuilding; the seams down either side remain visible beneath the shifting growth.

**Watching the top of the blockage — either aspect:** in Effulgent, a hairline contact marks where the arch rests on the slab. In Umbral, each movement of a pillar presses the arch against the slab and crushes a few tips. If someone asks what supports the arch, tell them **the pillars and the central slab both appear to bear its weight**.

**Looking through the notch — either aspect**

> Beyond the crystal, the paving continues a short way before ending at a gulf. Pale square shapes stand out over the depth, one beyond another. Nothing is moving toward you from the other side.

**Walking around:** there is no hidden wall or unseen barrier beside the arch. A character can inspect its back. The same slab and seams are visible there; no latch or separate mechanism is concealed behind it. A workable bypass remains allowed, as described below.

### First contact and visible reactions

**DM NOW:** Ask which piece they touch and what they try. A curious touch reveals the response; they do not need to announce an unknown power. Effulgent contact tends to align and hold the touched piece. Umbral contact loosens it. These are the existing puzzle effects, not new character abilities. A quick touch previews the change; sustained contact or a deliberate attempt carries it through.

**Effulgent touches a pillar — read to that character**

> Beneath your palm, the two ends of a broken line meet. The crack becomes difficult to find. The crystal above your hand settles into an exact alignment, and the bright line where this side of the arch meets the slab grows thinner.

**At the same moment — Umbral observers**

> The pillar beside that voice stops grinding. Its loose sections draw together. Dust falls from the crack once, then stops. The opposite pillar still shifts.

**If the hand is removed before the opening is completed:** the hairline mismatch returns in Effulgent; the seam begins grinding again in Umbral. Describe the change immediately. Stabilizing one side does not stabilize the other.

**Umbral loosens the center — read to that character**

> The points beneath your hand separate. A hollow spreads through the slab, and this time the new growth does not fill it. Through the thinning crystal, you begin to see the paving on the other side.

**At the same moment — Effulgent observers**

> A section of the smooth slab is gone. Light reaches through where your reflection had been. The opening widens, exposing the underside of the arch.

**If either pillar is still loose — everyone**

> The arch drops a finger’s breadth on its unsupported side. A sharp crack runs through the remaining crystal. Points thrust back into the opening until the slab is solid again and the arch rests against it.

**DM:** show the settling before anyone commits to passing underneath. This is the reset and the clue, not surprise crushing damage. If one pillar was held, only the other side visibly dips. The group can identify what still needs attention.

**Effulgent stabilizes the center:** its small flaws close; the reflection becomes clearer and the slab presses more completely against the arch. Umbral observers see its shedding surface stop breaking apart. The way remains blocked. Releasing contact restores the starting behavior.

**Umbral loosens a pillar:** its lower sections crumble and the arch tilts onto the central slab. Effulgent observers see the pillar’s aligned bands separate. Once the character stops, fragments regather into the original unstable pillar. Explain that the center is still blocking the way and now taking more of the weight.

**Both pillars held while the center is released — Effulgent**

> The lines inside both pillars meet cleanly from base to crown. Between them, the slab thins until the path beyond is clear. The arch remains exactly where it was. When you lift your hand, the alignment holds.

**The same success — Umbral**

> The growth parts beneath your touch and falls away in small fragments. This time, nothing pushes up to replace it. The pillars hold, the grinding stops, and the arch stands over an open passage.

**DM:** the completed arch stays stable until everyone is through, including the characters who held the pillars. Let them recognize that success; do not demand another action or secret phrase.

| Action | Immediate feedback and state |
| --- | --- |
| Effulgent character rests a hand on a side pillar | Its fractures align and the arch above it becomes still in both aspects. That support remains stable while contact continues. One person can hold one pillar; the two are 10 feet apart. |

**ASK / WAIT after feedback:** “What do you change?” Do not ask them to state that balance is important. Their plan is the answer.

**Hint ladder — use only if needed:** first describe the arch shifting when a support loses contact; next show a palm-sized section becoming steady under an Effulgent hand; finally ask whether they want to try holding the sides while loosening the middle. Clear intention succeeds without an ability check.

**If they bypass with climbing, tools, or magic:** let a workable plan succeed. Show the same touch reactions on a small accessible crystal on the far side so they can learn the interaction before the bridge. Do not negate a spell to preserve the lesson.

## 4. The moving bridge

> [!abstract] Puzzle at a glance — DM only
> **Description:** Five gaps separate the party from the far bank, but they have only three reusable bridge spans.
> **Design:** A movement and resource puzzle. Effulgent characters secure spans ahead; Umbral characters release empty spans behind. Players coordinate positions and carry recovered pieces forward without stranding companions.
> **Winning condition:** Get everyone to landing 5. Recycle spans to complete the crossing, or accept another workable traversal plan; no particular construction order is required.

**DM NOW:** This is a shared moving-platform problem. Three reusable spans must cover five gaps. Effulgent characters secure spans; Umbral characters release an empty span so it can be reused. Moving ahead without coordinating can strand someone, but they can restore the route behind them. Do not introduce additional secret bridge rules.

### Place the pieces before narrating

Draw six safe landings in a straight line, labeled **0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5**. Landing 0 is a 15-foot-square patch of the near bank; 5 is the matching patch of the far bank. Between them are **four freestanding platforms**, each 15 feet square. Every edge-to-edge gap is 20 feet. The distance from the near brink to the far brink is therefore 160 feet. Each landing holds the whole party.

Put three unused strips at the right-hand edge of landing 0. Each appears as a folded length of material about five feet long and a foot wide; placed across a gap, it unfolds into a walkway 20 feet long and five feet wide. This describes the existing reusable span mechanic. All character tokens begin on 0. The garden entrance is visible straight ahead on the far bank.

```text
START [0] --gap-- [1] --gap-- [2] --gap-- [3] --gap-- [4] --gap-- [5] GARDEN
Supply: three spans. A strip can connect only neighboring landings.
```

**READ ALOUD — Effulgent**

> The paving ends above a depth of white haze. Four pale platforms hang between you and the far bank, their square edges sharp against the emptiness. Nothing sways. Nothing falls.
>
> Faint outlines reach across the gaps, tracing a walkway that is not there. Three folded lengths of clear material lie at your feet on the right. Beyond the last platform, an opening in a low wall leads into another garden.

**READ ALOUD — Umbral**

> The paving breaks off above a gulf of drifting stone. Four broad platforms stretch toward the far bank. Grains sift from their edges and disappear among the fragments below, but their upper surfaces hold.
>
> Three dark strips lie folded at your feet on the right. Along their edges, fibers part and weave themselves together again. Across the gulf, a weathered wall encloses a garden; its entrance faces the broken crossing.

**Tell them when they examine the site:** there are five gaps and only three spans. The landings are safe places to stop. Securing a span produces the same steadiness as holding a pillar; loosening one makes it available again. Let players plan with the actual diagram.

### Player actions and feedback

Use freely described actions, not combat rounds. Resolve one meaningful action at a time, alternating invitations between aspects. Everyone may scout, carry, cross, and propose a plan; specialized actions follow the table below.

| Action | Procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Place | Any character at a landing places an available span across an adjacent gap. It remains slack and unsafe until secured. The spans are light enough for one character to carry. |
| Secure — Effulgent | An Effulgent character touching an end fixes the span to both neighboring landings. Show it becoming still. No concentration or continuous touch required. |
| Cross | Any character crosses a secured span, moves their token, and states what they carry. No roll. A landing holds everyone. |
| Release — Umbral | An Umbral character touching an end releases a span **only when nobody is on it**. It folds into a carryable strip at that character’s landing. Remove it from the diagram and put it in that character’s inventory. |
| Release occupied span | It draws taut around the traveler’s weight and refuses to fold. No fall and no lost piece. Explain the obstruction so they can correct it. |
| Cross unsecured span | It sags before the traveler commits their weight. Give the warning and a chance to secure it or turn back. Do not spring a damage check on a misunderstood rule. |

**Important logistics:** released strips do not teleport to the front. A character must carry one along the existing route to the next unbridged gap. If they isolate someone, replace and secure a span to reconnect the landing. Tokens on a span must finish crossing before that strip is released.

### Worked route — DM reference only

1. Place and secure spans across 0–1, 1–2, and 2–3, keeping characters able to reach each working end.
2. Move everyone off landing 0 and onto landing 1 or beyond. An Umbral character on 1 releases 0–1.
3. Carry that strip along 1–2–3; place and secure it across 3–4.
4. Move everyone off landing 1 and onto 2 or beyond. An Umbral character on 2 releases 1–2.
5. Carry it to 4; place and secure 4–5. Everyone crosses to 5.

They can use only two spans or another valid order. Do not require the worked route. The limited supply and the party’s positions create the puzzle; no hidden correct emblem, timer, or philosophical speech is required.

**If stalled:** point at the empty span behind them and ask what changed when Umbral hands loosened the arch. If separated, show precisely which gap breaks their route. Let them recover it.

**If a character actually falls through an unusual action:** for this puzzle draft, the gulf returns the soul to the last landing they occupied, with carried puzzle pieces, after a disorienting moment. This is a local reset, not a general immunity to death. Do not spend resources or impose damage for this reset.

**If they fly, teleport, jump, or rig a rope:** adjudicate normally; these gaps have no blanket anti-magic rule. A character reaching 5 can help others across. If a valid plan bypasses the entire bridge, let it. The garden still offers the return route.

**NEXT:** once the group reaches 5, [[#5. The seasonal garden]]. Do not force a bonus gap or another bridge behind their success.

## 5. The seasonal garden

> [!abstract] Puzzle at a glance — DM only
> **Description:** Three planting beds begin at different stages of a repeating growth cycle; a sealed doorway responds when all three flower together.
> **Design:** A timing puzzle with player-triggered pulses. Effulgent characters grow or hold a bed; Umbral characters release later stages, including restarting exhausted soil. Players coordinate changes and pauses rather than permanently preserving or destroying everything.
> **Winning condition:** All three beds must be at **Bloom after the same pulse**. This opens the route home; no extra offering or spoken answer is needed.

**DM NOW — goal:** get all three beds to **Bloom at the end of the same pulse**. Effulgent can encourage or hold a state; Umbral can release a finished state into the next. Soil needs an Umbral release to begin again. Everything is visible and reversible.

### Set the board

Draw a courtyard 40 feet square, entered from the south. Its stone wall is eight feet high. A sealed doorway, five feet wide and eight feet high, is centered in the opposite, northern wall.

Place three six-foot-square raised beds around the center: **Pool** on the left/west, **Arch** on the right/east, and **Wall** ahead/north. A small round water basin sits between the Pool bed and the western wall. A head-high trellis arch stands over the Arch bed; this is a garden trellis, not the earlier crystal arch. The Wall bed stands ten feet short of the exit wall. Five-foot-wide paths let characters reach every bed and pass around the northern bed to the doorway.

The pulse bowl rests on a waist-high stone pedestal at the center, between the beds. The small bowl holds no water and is distinct from the western water basin. Explain the bed labels through those landmarks; players do not need to infer names from private DM notes.

```text
                NORTH WALL / SEALED DOOR
                         [Wall bed]

water basin   [Pool bed]     bowl     [Arch bed + trellis]

                      SOUTH ENTRANCE
                        FROM BRIDGE
```

Place five stage labels in this order:

**Seed → Shoot → Bloom → Fruit → Soil → Seed.**

Set **Pool = Bloom; Arch = Fruit; Wall = Seed**. Put a palm-sized stone bowl on the central pedestal. Touching its rim together voluntarily triggers one pulse; there is no real-time countdown. Show players the stage track once they inspect a bed. Nobody needs botanical knowledge.

**READ ALOUD — Effulgent**

> The path brings you into a walled garden. To your left, flowers stand beside a round basin, their reflections as still as the blossoms themselves. On your right, ripe fruit hangs beneath an arched trellis. Ahead, seeds lie in open furrows, each nestled in an identical hollow.
>
> A shallow bowl waits on a pedestal between the three beds. Beyond the seeds, the far wall bears the outline of a doorway. Smooth stone fills the outline without a seam.

**READ ALOUD — Umbral**

> The path brings you into a walled garden, smelling of damp soil and overripe fruit. Beside a basin on your left, petals curl at their edges. Vines bow beneath the fruit hanging from a trellis on your right. Ahead, seeds shift in cracked furrows, not yet sprouting.
>
> Fine roots work between the stones around the beds. At their center stands a pedestal with a shallow bowl. Beyond the seeds, mortar trickles down a door-shaped outline in the wall. The stone within it still holds.

**Inspecting the bowl:** five pictures circle its rim in clockwise order: a seed, a two-leaf shoot, an open flower, a fruit, and bare soil. A continuous groove links the soil picture back to the seed. On the pedestal’s north face, directly facing the sealed doorway, three flowers sit side by side above a small picture of an open door. Explain the objective plainly once examined: the door responds to **three flowering beds together**, not permanent stillness or clearing everything away.

### Run each pulse in this exact order

1. **Show current stages.** Ask each player what they try; explain legal touch effects below when discovered, without checks.
2. **Choose actions.** Each character can act on at most one bed. Each bed receives at most one action for this pulse; other characters can examine, coordinate, or operate the bowl. Players may revise conflicting choices before committing.
3. **Apply chosen actions simultaneously.** Move markers as described below.
4. **Pulse:** every bed not Held advances **one** stage, except Soil stays Soil. A Held bed does not advance. Holding expires after this pulse.
5. **Check the result:** if all three are Bloom, the door opens. Otherwise describe the new states and start the next pulse. No damage, lost seed stock, or irreversible withering occurs.

| Action | Available to | Change before the pulse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grow | Effulgent | Seed → Shoot, or Shoot → Bloom. Does not skip other stages. The bed still advances during the pulse unless it was Held, which would be a different action. |
| Hold | Effulgent | Keep this bed in its current stage through this pulse. One character holds one bed. |
| Release | Umbral | Bloom → Fruit, Fruit → Soil, or Soil → Seed. The bed still receives its normal pulse afterward; Soil is the exception that stays Soil. |
| Wait | Anyone | No pre-pulse change; the bed receives its normal pulse. |

**Both aspects must contribute:** Effulgent can preserve useful stages but cannot restart Soil. Umbral can release exhausted growth but cannot hold a flowering bed still. A third character on either side adds flexibility; neither group needs exactly three members.

**Effulgent feedback:** held forms become perfectly still; changes appear as a new complete arrangement when attention returns after the bowl’s pulse. **Umbral feedback:** show ripening, collapse, roots, and emergence. These are two perceptions of the same markers; never tell the groups different mechanical stages.

### Worked solution — valid with two characters on either side

| Pulse | Pool | Arch | Wall | Result after pulse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Bloom | Fruit | Seed | — |
| 1 | Effulgent Hold | Wait | Wait | Bloom / Soil / Shoot |
| 2 | Effulgent Hold | Umbral Release: Soil → Seed | Wait | Bloom / Shoot / Bloom |
| 3 | Effulgent Hold | Wait | Second Effulgent Hold | Bloom / Bloom / Bloom: open |

Other routes count. A Grow action can overshoot Bloom at the pulse; show that result accurately rather than quietly changing the timing to rescue a chosen answer.

**Hints:** first show that unattended fruit becomes soil while soil itself waits. Next ask which bed is already where they need it. Finally explain that a held flower can wait for the others. If players want a reset, they may deliberately clear the board and restore the starting arrangement by tracing the five marks on the bowl; this explicit reset has no cost.

**If they force the door or use magic:** it is an image of the blocked return, not an ordinary door hiding another room. Explain this when inspected. Let relevant plant or preservation magic accomplish an appropriate listed action without additional resource taxes; adjudicate a genuine planar escape spell on its own terms. Do not invent universal immunity to magic.

## Unexpected choices

**Nobody eats:** no purgatory scene. Return to [[Sessions/Potential Events/The Two Truths v2 - Elder and Resolution#7. Investigate the supply|investigating the supply]] or the party’s chosen course. The elder can negotiate but does not dose them secretly.

**Only some eat:** send only those characters. Alternate brief material-world scenes for the others: seeing Oren return, fetching Vetch, helping move bodies, questioning the elder. Do not describe their absent souls as participating. If an aspect has no player, do not run a cooperative puzzle with an unavailable required action: offer remaining players control of clearly identified temporary puzzle echoes, if they want that, or resolve that scene as sensory exploration rather than a locked challenge. Echoes are table aids, not new companions or a change to where PCs traveled. With one Effulgent participant, allow a crystal support or held bed to retain the effect while that person attends a second; explain that participation adjustment before play, not after failure.

**Players stall:** after two attempts without new information, make the feedback more explicit. If frustration persists, offer the next hint. No real-time pressure is built into these puzzles. They need not state the theme, make a personal confession, or accept a patron to progress.

**Players use unexpected abilities:** allow ordinary movement and utility magic where relevant. A bypass is a result, not a failure of the scene. Mark consumed resources; nothing here grants a free rest. For an unusual effect on the separated bodies or souls that the campaign has not defined, pause to make a consistent table ruling instead of announcing an invented universal law.

**Players become hostile to each other or attempt dangerous acts outside the procedures:** use normal table adjudication and the actual declared action. The puzzle-reset provisions are narrow; they do not establish that death, injury, or all forms of violence are impossible in purgatory. Do not add punitive monsters to enforce cooperation.

**After play:** record actual participants, Naieht’s side, route, resource use, return order, material-world time if established, and what the characters believe they learned. Keep proposed mechanics distinct from newly recorded events.
