## Room 2 — The Threshold

> The star-woven crossing brings you to the mouth of a garden path. Everything is almost too much to look at directly — not bright exactly, just *saturated*, the way a color looks after rain. The air smells like warm bread, cut grass, and, underneath that, something floral you can't quite name. Somewhere off to your left, a bird is singing four notes over and over, pleased with itself. The path is lined with hedges trimmed into shapes that might be animals, or might just be hedges, cut with more care than any hedge has ever gotten.
>
> Blocking the path is a gate. Wrought in pale gold metal, twisted into looping vines — and *alive:* actual vines have grown through and around the metalwork so thoroughly you can't tell where the forge work ends and the garden begins. In the center of the gate, four flowers are set in a ring, each a different bloom. Beneath them, carved into a stone lintel in flowing script, a few lines of verse — weathered, and only every other line is legible:
>
> *"First, the light that wakes the seed... ... ... then, the hush that dims the day... ..."*

**The ring (clockwise from the top, as they face the gate):**
- **Top** — a wide-open golden bloom, round as a coin, its face lifted like it's drinking the sun.
- **Right** — a dark violet night-flower with a silver heart, its edges closed as if for sleeping.
- **Bottom** — a pale gold **bud**, half-open, petals folded like praying hands.
- **Left** — a rose-colored bloom whose petals are slowly, visibly **folding shut.**

**What this table can work out:** the bud is the *"light that wakes the seed"* (Dawn), and the closing bloom is the *"hush that dims the day"* (Dusk). Line 1 comes before line 3. That's all they have. The missing lines (*"then, the warmth that feeds the need"* and *"last, the dark where lost things stay"*) are legible on the Umbral side — they'll have to shout across the wall for them.

**The full solution:** **Bottom → Top → Left → Right** (bud, golden bloom, closing bloom, night-flower). Touching each in order makes it glow warm. **A wrong touch makes all four fold in and go dim,** with a soft, dull *clunk* the other table can hear. **The gate opens only when both tables have finished** — vines and all, it simply stops being there.

*Texture to drop in if they linger:* a fat bumblebee that's been circling the whole time and never once lands; the hedge-animals seem to have turned to face wherever the party's standing, though no one saw them move; a single perfect apple sitting in the grass, untouched, that nobody should eat (it's not a trap, it's just not relevant — let them worry about it for nothing).

## Room 3 — The Tapestry and the Door

> The path opens into a round chamber, walled in warm honey-colored stone, open to a soft twilight sky through a gap in the ceiling far overhead. On the far wall hangs an enormous **tapestry** — woven so finely it could be a window instead of cloth. It shows a court in mid-dance: fae in bright silk, a ring of lantern-light, a couple at the center caught mid-turn, laughing at something just out of frame. The colors are so rich you could believe the dancers are about to finish the step.

**If they take the tapestry down** (it's just hung on hooks, heavy but not fixed; no trap, no lock):

> You haul the tapestry off its hooks in a long, slow slide of silk and dust. Behind it: honey-colored stone. Plain, warm, unbroken. No door, no seam, no hidden latch. Just wall, the exact color of the room around it.

This is the intended dead end. Let them search, knock, and check for hidden switches. Nothing responds. Let them sit with it until they think to ask the other room what *they're* looking at.

**Sound cue when Umbral forces their door open** (read this the moment your co-DM signals):

> From somewhere behind the wall — muffled, but unmistakable — a sharp *crack* of splitting wood, like a door giving way. The tapestry, or the bare stone where it hung, shivers as though a draft just moved through it.

**Then:**

> The tapestry billows outward, once, like a held breath finally let go — or, if it's already down, the bare wall simply *opens,* the stone parting on a seam that was never there — and there's an archway. Easy, unremarkable, as if it had always been there.

**If the tapestry is still up when the door opens,** add: the dancers haven't moved. The couple at the center is still mid-turn. But you'd swear the woman's eyes are pointed differently than they were.

*Texture:* faint music seems to come from the tapestry itself, always just resolving into a real tune and never quite getting there; the honey-stone walls are warm to the touch, almost body-temperature; if anyone examines the dancers closely, one background figure near the edge of the weave is facing directly *out* of the tapestry, toward the party, in a scene where everyone else is facing inward. This means nothing. Let them chase it.

## Room 4 — The Herd

**The Herd Key and riddle answers are in [[The Two Truths#Room 4 — The Herd]].** Below is what *this table sees* and the Shepherd's riddles in full.

> Beyond the archway, the path opens onto a wide, sun-warmed meadow — the kind of green that looks freshly painted. Wildflowers everywhere, lazy and low. Six sheep are scattered across it, each near something — a great **willow** far off to the left, a **flat stone** in the middle of the meadow, a **broken fence** near on your left, a small clear **pond** near on your right, an **overturned hay cart** far off to the right, and a tall **post with a brass bell** near the far end of the meadow, by the path that leads onward.
>
> Just inside the meadow, on a three-legged stool, sits an old **shepherd** — barefoot, a crook taller than he is, a wide-brimmed hat woven from the same grass underfoot. He looks up as you arrive and smiles like you're expected.
>
> *"Ah — travelers. Lovely day for it. I've a few small riddles for you, if you've time. I do so love a good riddle over an idle afternoon."*

**The sheep (this table's view — each DM detail is free to give when asked; do not volunteer all of it at once):**

| At | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Willow** (far left) | **Black** fleece · **brass bell** round its neck · dozing under the trailing branches. *Flavor:* burrs tangled in its wool. |
| **Flat Stone** (center) | **White** fleece · **blue ribbon** on its left ear · chewing slowly. *Flavor:* a small scar on its nose. |
| **Fence** (near left) | **Grey** fleece · a **crown of red poppies** · staring steadily at the party. *Flavor:* one ear is notched. |
| **Pond** (near right) | **Cream** fleece · nothing worn · drinking at the water's edge. *Flavor:* muddy to the knees. |
| **Cart** (far right) | **Dark brown** fleece · a **tin bell** on a cord · lying in the cart's shade. *Flavor:* bits of hay caught in its wool. |
| **Bell Post** (far center) | **Piebald** (grey-and-white patches) fleece · a **yellow ribbon** round its neck · standing beside the post. *Flavor:* a chipped hoof; it looks very pleased with itself. |

**No riddle here can be solved by looking at the sheep alone.** Each one turns on something **hidden beneath the wool** that only the Umbral table can see. That's the intent. The Shepherd tells them so, gently, in the first riddle.

**The Shepherd's riddles** (deliver in order; give the *plain version* after a wrong answer or a long silence):

**U1 — The Whitest Coat**
> *"Each of us wears a second hide, stolen or grown or worn with pride. Some are dark and some are dun — which of us wears the whitest one?"*

*Plain:* Each redcap has a fleece-coat you can't see. Which is the **whitest?** **Answer: the Stone.**

**E1 — The Crown Beneath**
> *"Wool's the half of any sheep; the rest is hid where wool runs deep. Every one of mine wears red — some worn pale, some worn dead. Point me to the deepest-dyed, the crown that's soaked the most inside."*

*Plain:* Each sheep has a red cap under its wool. Which cap is the **deepest, darkest red?** **Answer: the Pond.**

**U2 — Ribbon and Hook**
> *"Ribbons tied on two of ours; one is handy with a hook. Point me to the ribboned one who has the hooked blade — take a look."*

*Plain:* Two have ribbons. Only one of them carries a **hooked blade.** **Answer: the Bell Post.** (The Willow's sickle is hooked too, but it has no ribbon.)

**E2 — Bell and Iron**
> *"Two of mine will ring as they roam; one of the two goes iron-shod home. Not the one that treads on leather — bring me the one with bell and iron, together."*

*Plain:* Two sheep wear bells. Only one of those two has **iron boots.** **Answer: the Cart.** (The Willow's bell-wearer has leather boots.)



**When a riddle is answered correctly:** the named sheep steps forward, dips its head, and steps back. **When it's wrong:** the shepherd tuts, restates the plain version, and adds: *"Have you asked the folk beside you what they see?"*

**Noise cues from the Umbral table** (weave them into the scene without breaking tone): if you hear shouting through the wall about caps, boots, or hooks, the shepherd notices with mild interest: *"Oh, they've gone and found my little secret. Good."* He's a warm figure, not a coy one — he wants them to figure it out.



**Resolution:** once both tables have finished, the **brass bell** on the post rings once, clear and warm, and the flock trots off down the path in an easy line. The way forward is open.

*Texture:* the sheep smell of lanolin and warm grass; a lazy breeze moves through the wildflowers; the shepherd whittles a small crook as he waits, humming very softly, and will hand a finished one to a player who asks (it's a nice little prop and it means nothing); the flock's bells, when they move, make a sound like distant laughter.

## Room 5 — The Shrine of Two Mothers

> Beyond the herd, the path narrows into a small grove, hushed and green-gold, centered on a raised stone altar. Atop it, a statue: a tall, winged matron, robes carved so finely they seem to move, arms outstretched and open. A pair of doves nest in the crook of one carved arm, cooing softly, entirely undisturbed by your arrival. Incense burns in a shallow dish at her feet — sweet, warm, something like honeysuckle and beeswax.
>
> There's no instruction carved anywhere. Just the statue, waiting, open-armed, the way you'd wait for someone to come home.

**If anyone examines the base of the statue, worn words are still legible:**

> *"Bring what still lives. Bring it freely, or bring nothing at all."*

It's not a riddle. If a player asks what it means, that's exactly what it means: something alive — a lock of hair, a flower, a kind word meant sincerely — given because they want to, not because they're told to.

**The puzzle:** the statue wants an offering given **freely and still living** — a lock of hair, a produced flower, a minor cantrip of light or growth, a sincerely spoken kind word to the statue itself. Let each player choose their own; don't prescribe one. Once everyone's ready, coordinate a countdown across the wall with the Umbral table ("on three") — **both rooms must offer at the same moment.**

**If a player asks who she is:** nobody answers. If anyone gets too hung up on it, the doves coo, the incense curls, and the moment moves on. She's simply the **Mother of Both Halves** and the dream doesn't explain her.

**When both offerings land simultaneously:**

> The doves lift off together, circle once, and vanish into the leaf-light overhead. The statue's stone hands close, gently, as if accepting something too small to see — and the gold in the grove **softens.** It doesn't fade so much as *warm,* deepening into the color of lantern-glow, and the smell of honeysuckle thickens until it's the smell of spiced wine and woodsmoke. You feel very, very tired, in the way you feel at the end of a long, good evening.

Then cue the wake-up (see below).

*Texture:* the incense smoke, if watched closely, seems to briefly form shapes — a wing, a face, a door — before dissolving; the altar stone is worn smooth in the exact shape of countless hands that came before, in a spot none of the party has touched yet.
