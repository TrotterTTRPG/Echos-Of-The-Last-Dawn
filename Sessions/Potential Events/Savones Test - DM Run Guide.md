## Start here

**Read these six facts before speaking as Savone. Everything else appears when needed.**

1. **The job:** recover South Road Couriers’ blue ledger from Merrit, its former bookkeeper, and deliver it to Edda, the new owner. Merrit claims the previous owner owes him wages. Edda is not the previous owner.
2. **The complication:** Merrit tried selling the ledger to Hollis. Hollis attempted to take it without paying. Merrit is now barricaded inside an old parcel depot with the book.
3. **The secret:** the entire dispute is staged by Savone. Merrit, Hollis, Rell, Doss, Nella, and Edda are all his people. The ledger contains invented clients. Ordinary bystanders and the real Watch are not actors.
4. **Savone’s reason:** Po asked about the Gentleman. Savone warned her away; he likes her and is loyal to her mentor Faris. Before exposing his connections, he needs evidence that her companions are capable, discreet, and truthful. His rank and exact power remain undefined.
5. **Real opposition, real limits:** the crew tries to win, accepts defeat, and uses melee knockouts. They never attack unconscious characters or require a fight to the death. **If the deception is exposed, the Watch arrives, or someone faces death, an operative calls “The account is closed,” stops fighting, and reveals Savone arranged this.** Then go to [[#The setup is exposed]]. No one has immunity from injury or magic.
6. **Follow actual events:** the ledger starts in Merrit’s satchel in room C. Move it only when someone actually obtains it. Let stealth, negotiation, or magic skip obstacles. Actors know only what they can perceive or have been told; Savone receives reports afterward.

**Use these written terms unless changed before the opening:** 150 gp party fee on delivery, plus a 60 gp negotiation advance; return anything unspent. These are the scenario’s proposed terms. Once offered, keep them consistent.

**Keep this tracker visible.** Start the depot positions when the party first scouts it. Everyone’s AC, actions, and modifiers are in [[#Combat desk]] if violence begins unexpectedly.

## 1. Savone offers the job

**DM NOW:** Savone presents ordinary work, not a test. He appears to know about the wage dispute and an earlier failed collection; he does not reveal Hollis or the attempted sale. His carpet stall is in Thalyra’s Bronze District. Every job location is elsewhere. Voice: measured, practical, familiar with Po; not theatrically mysterious.

**READ ALOUD**

> Savone waits until a browsing customer moves on. He closes his book and lays a folded set of papers on the counter.

**SAVONE**

> “You asked about work. Edda, someone I deal with, has bought South Road Couriers. Some of their business is respectable. Some would interest the Watch.”
>
> “Their old bookkeeper, Merrit, left with the accounts. He says the previous owner owes him wages. Edda disputes the amount and says it isn’t her debt. The last person she sent came back bruised and empty-handed.”
>
> “Get the book back before she sends someone who makes a worse mess. Deliver it to Edda. Keep the business private, and be reasonable with Merrit. He’s worth more alive.”
>
> “A hundred and fifty for the group. Sixty to negotiate with; bring back what you don’t spend. Ask before promising more. And keep my name out of it.”

**Give the players these facts now:**

- Merrit rents rooms above **the Kneaded Rest**, a bakery. The outside stair is in its rear courtyard.
- Edda’s office is at the **South Road Couriers freight yard**, several streets away from Savone’s business. Give clear street directions to both addresses.
- Ledger: **blue cloth, split lower spine, South Road’s stamp inside the cover**. A leather satchel is not itself proof of identity.
- Papers: Edda’s collection authorization and sale extract showing the records were included. These contain no Savone or Moonhelm identifier.
- Edda will receive the book herself. For table identification, her office bears the firm’s sign and she has the matching copy of the authorization. Tell them that now.

**If asked…**

| Question | Savone’s answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why us? | “One person has already been turned away. I need people who can handle resistance without starting a street war.” |
| Why not the Watch? | “The book records the sort of deliveries people pay to keep private. Edda cannot complain without explaining those.” |
| Can we inspect it? | “Enough to know what you’re carrying. Keep what’s inside private.” |
| Can we hurt Merrit? | “Bring the book back. Don’t kill a useful man over disputed wages.” |
| Does this get Po information on the Gentleman? | “I told you what I thought of that. This is work I can offer you.” No promise of access yet. |

**ASK / WAIT:** “What do you want to clarify, and where do you go first?”

**GO TO:** Merrit’s address → [[#2. The Kneaded Rest]]. Edda first → she confirms ownership, says Hollis has asked to buy the accounts, and supplies the old depot’s address; proceed to [[#3. Arriving at the depot]]. Decline → Savone accepts the answer; end this mission without punishment.

## 2. The Kneaded Rest

**DM NOW:** Merrit left voluntarily with the ledger to meet Hollis. His room is empty; a letter gives the destination without a check. Bakery staff are ordinary residents, not secret examiners. This stop should take only a few minutes of table time.

**READ ALOUD**

> Oven warmth follows you into the rear courtyard. Above the bakery, a door stands ajar. Inside, folded clothes sit beside an open traveling bag. A cup has tipped across a desk, soaking the edge of a letter.

**When they examine the desk, give them this letter:**

> Merrit—
>
> Bring the South Road account book to the old parcel depot beside their freight yard. I will meet you on the sorting floor. If the records are what you say, we can settle your price there.
>
> Hollis

A simple sketch on its reverse identifies the depot. **No roll for the letter, its signature, or its directions.** No ledger is here.

**If they investigate:** DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) identifies hurried packing rather than a struggle: undamaged latch, unscattered belongings, untouched purse. Patient examination reaches the same conclusion. A failed roll leaves the reason for departure uncertain, not the destination.

**If they question residents:** a witness may have seen Merrit leave carrying a wrapped book. Residents cannot explain the sale, identify Savone, or solve the mission for them.

**ASK / WAIT:** “You have the meeting place. Do you head there, or do something else first?”

**GO TO:** follow letter → [[#3. Arriving at the depot]]. Return to Edda → she confirms Hollis has expressed interest and points to the same depot. Return to Savone → he tells them to investigate the new lead and report what they find; he does not reveal the setup.

## 3. Arriving at the depot

**DM NOW:** The depot is an older overflow building beside the active freight yard. Hollis supposedly rented it for a private purchase. Merrit worked here and knew where to barricade himself. The crew has four members; no reinforcements are waiting. Put the starting positions from the tracker in place now.

**READ ALOUD**

> The old depot’s broad doors stand almost closed. A man watches through the gap. Dusty windows overlook the courtyard from a timber gallery. Around the side, a ramp slopes down to a metal loading shutter. From inside comes a pry bar’s scrape, then a voice: “Break in here and you’ll get nothing.”

**Tell anyone looking:** the main door is guarded; a person moves behind the gallery windows; the ramp is a third approach. No roll is needed to see the entrances. The shouting is Merrit, the scraping Doss. Do not name unseen people to the players yet.

### Establish the space before they commit

Draw four labeled areas. This is a connection sketch, not a scale battle map:

```text
                    COURTYARD WINDOWS
                            |
                  B: UPPER GALLERY (+10 ft)
                   / stairs | ladder through hatch
                  /         |
STREET -- A: SORTING --door-- C: RECORDS ROOM (street level)
                  \         |
                   \ stairs | service stair
                    D: LOWER LOADING (-10 ft) -- shutter -- RAMP

A cargo hoist also connects D to B; its winch is at B.
```

Footprint: about 60 × 40 feet. Stairs and corridors: 5 feet wide. Loading ramp: 10 feet wide. For immediate play, place C at the rear of A, the main entrance opposite it, and crate stacks between them. Mark the stairs and hoist before movement matters, then keep their positions fixed. Daylight lights A/B; daylight through a small ramp grille leaves D dim. These are practical staging defaults.

### Resolve their chosen entrance

| Approach | What the DM does now | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main door | Rell: “Private meeting. What’s your business?” Papers identify them as Edda’s collectors, not Hollis’s guests. He calls Hollis rather than automatically admitting them. A credible freight-business bluff may work: uncertain Deception opposed by Rell’s Insight +0. | [[#A. Sorting floor]]. |
| Upper windows | Sill 15 feet above courtyard. Hurried unaided climb: Athletics DC 12. Secured rope or suitable magic can remove the roll. Quiet latch opening: Dexterity with thieves’ tools DC 13. Breaking glass opens it noisily. | [[#B. Gallery]]. |
| Loading ramp | Quiet exterior lock: Dexterity with thieves’ tools DC 14. Force it: Athletics DC 16, noisy. Failure leaves it closed and costs time; no surprise guards spawn. | [[#D. Lower loading passage]]. |
| Scout / use magic | Describe real positions within sight. Nella has passive Perception 16; other crew 10. Walls still block sight. Apply the actual spell and normal hiding rules. | Reveal only what they could learn, then ask for their approach. |

**Do not start combat just for a failed check.** Decide who heard or saw the attempt, update the alert tracker, and have that person respond. For violence at any entrance, go directly to [[#Combat desk]].

### If they delay outside

Allow the first plan without spending time. Thereafter, approximately ten minutes of sustained in-world delay advances one stage. Player discussion and single checks are not automatically ten minutes.

| Stage | Announce | Actual change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pressure — start | Metal scrapes; the door creaks. | Doss works at C’s door; everyone remains in starting position. |
| Breach — next | A crack of wood, a shout, footsteps descending. | Merrit carries the book down C’s service stair into D. Crew follows. |
| Removal — next | The loading shutter rolls up; a cart rattles toward the ramp. | If still unopposed, Hollis has seized the book. Crew leaves on foot with a handcart; Merrit is bound in D. |

**Once the party intervenes, stop automatic stage advances.** Resolve movement and custody normally. They can intercept a breach or departure. Crew already leaving → [[#If the crew escapes with the book]].

## 4. Inside the depot

Use only the room the party enters. Each card contains the facts needed there. An NPC who moved stays moved; these descriptions are starting conditions, not resets.

### A. Sorting floor

**DM NOW:** Rell is at the street entrance; Hollis and Doss are at the rear records-room door. Nella watches from B above. Crates provide broken sightlines. Stairs lead up to B and down to D. Merrit and the ledger are behind C’s barricade.

**READ ALOUD**

> Dust catches the light between stacks of shipping crates. At the back, a man works a pry bar into a closed door while another speaks through it. Timber stairs rise to the gallery; a second stair drops below the floor.

**HOLLIS — if he notices collectors**

> “Merrit offered me that book. Now he’s bringing Edda into our meeting? Leave it here and you can take him.”

**His aim:** obtain the ledger and retain a way out. He knows the party’s identities only if told or recognized; he cannot see concealed actions from behind crates. He buys conversation time while Doss continues unless someone stops Doss.

**To negotiate:** credible leverage can win a settlement. The 60 gp advance plus an obvious risk of defeat can persuade him to leave. If clearly beaten, he may leave for nothing. Savone’s name is never required. An unconvincing demand gets refusal, not instant violence.

**To maneuver:** a handcart can block a 5-foot aisle with an action; anyone can move or climb over it. Crates give half or three-quarters cover according to how much of a target they actually obscure. Choose and announce the cover before an attack.

**If they force past Rell or take the book through an exit the crew controls:** warn “Leave the book.” Continued resistance → [[#Combat desk]]. Reach C → [[#C. Merrit and the ledger]]. Reach another floor → its room card.

### B. Gallery

**DM NOW:** Nella is the lookout, not a mandatory conversation. She begins patrolling the window side. The gallery overlooks A, with stairs back to A, a hatch and ladder into C, and the winch for a hoist down to D.

**READ ALOUD**

> The gallery runs above the crate stacks. A timber hatch sits near its far end. Beside it, a winch feeds rope into a vertical cargo shaft. A woman pauses at the railing, listening to the argument below.

If Nella has moved or been distracted, omit her from the narration.

**If unseen:** allow access to the hatch, winch, or stairs without forcing a confrontation. The hatch opens with an action and the fixed ladder reaches C. No extra lock.

**If seen:** Nella calls the location to Hollis and moves to cover or a useful exit. She does not automatically know their purpose. If approached peacefully, she asks their business and relays it. If attacked, use [[#Combat desk]].

**Hoist:** platform begins at D. The winch here moves it 10 feet per action, carrying up to two Medium creatures; the full D-to-B trip is 20 feet and takes two actions. The brake holds between actions. It passes A’s height but has no opening onto A. Someone must stay at the winch unless a player devises another valid arrangement. This is a staging clarification of the scenario’s equipment, not a universal rule.

**NEXT:** hatch → [[#C. Merrit and the ledger]]; stairs → [[#A. Sorting floor]]; platform → [[#D. Lower loading passage]].

### C. Merrit and the ledger

**DM NOW:** Merrit has the only ledger, in his leather satchel. He tried selling it for 120 gp, Hollis tried taking it, and he fled here. His story is acted, but he really will hand over the book for a credible safe exit. He has 4 HP; do not treat him as a combatant or an invulnerable prop.

**MERRIT — if contacted before he trusts them**

> “If Hollis sent you, tell him I can still tear the names out.”

When they identify Edda, let him explain the failed sale, then:

> “Fine. Edda gets her book. But get me out with it. I’m not opening that door for them.”

**Give these room facts when visible:** a desk braces the door to A; a ceiling hatch and ladder connect to B; a service door opens onto a short stair down to D. Merrit can open either ground-level door from inside. He does not need to wait for Hollis to finish prying.

**Resolve their offer:** a believable escape plan is enough. He can pass the satchel up the ladder or cooperate at the service door. His wage demand may be settled with some of the advance, but do not require payment plus a persuasion roll after they have offered a convincing rescue.

**If they take by force:** use actual attacks or an appropriate contested attempt; possession does not change because the plot needs it. If forcing the barricade becomes necessary, use Athletics DC 16 as a noisy staging default; a failed attempt leaves it intact, permitting another approach. This is separate from Doss’s slow, unopposed prying clock.

**If they check the book:** blue cloth, split lower spine, South Road stamp. Entries mix ordinary deliveries with coded illicit business. There is no second ledger and no surprise replacement objective. The unusually fresh ink can raise suspicion; nothing identifies real clients.

**Merrit follows sensible directions:** he can walk, climb with assistance, Dash, Disengage, or Dodge. He does not run into danger to manufacture a rescue scene.

**ASK / WAIT:** “Which route do you use, and who carries the book?” Update custody, then [[#5. Getting out]]. Leaving him behind is possible; judge the actual risks and alternatives later.

### D. Lower loading passage

**DM NOW:** This area starts unoccupied. Stairs connect to A and C; a cargo platform connects to B. The exit shutter starts closed and locked from outside but can be opened from inside. It has not been drop-barred. Check the tracker for any changes before narrating.

**READ ALOUD**

> The passage opens beneath the sorting floor. A cargo platform rests under an open shaft. Light seeps through the grille beside a broad shutter at the end of the loading ramp. Two narrow stairs climb back into the building.

**Shutter:** from inside, one action releases the exterior lock and raises the shutter; another action lowers it. Dropping or lifting its interior bar takes a separate action. A cart wedged beneath prevents closure until moved. Tell players when someone starts closing it; it inflicts no surprise crushing damage.

**If barred after play begins:** the original DC 14 exterior lock check no longer opens the interior bar. Someone inside can lift it, or use the established DC 16 force option noisily. Alternate entrances remain usable.

**Hoist:** without a winch operator on B, the brake holds the platform. A rope, climb, or magic can provide another route up the shaft; adjudicate the actual method rather than declaring the shaft impassable.

**Exit clear?** They can leave. Do not teleport an enemy here. If someone observed them, move that pursuer from their actual location. Pursuit or interception → [[#5. Getting out]].

## 5. Getting out

**DM NOW:** Getting out with the correct book completes the dangerous part of the job. There is no required ambush after a clean escape. Crew members chase only what they can detect; nobody automatically identifies the satchel’s new carrier.

| Current situation | Run this now |
| --- | --- |
| Crew unaware; route clear | The party and any companion escape. Go to [[#6. Edda and Savone]]. |
| Seen leaving with ledger | Observer shouts the location. Reachable opponents intercept or pursue along actual routes. Demand the book before violence where possible. Use [[#Combat desk]] if timing becomes contested. |
| Hollis willing to bargain | Accept a credible deal: payment within authority, withdrawal, or surrender when beaten. Record promises. Let the agreement work. |
| Party hands ledger to Hollis | He accepts it and orders disengagement. They may recover it later or report failure truthfully. No forced betrayal of the bargain. |
| Party abandons book / retreats | Crew secures the book if it can reach it. Party can regroup; go to the escaped-book branch below if the crew leaves. |
| Serious injury or exposed setup | An operative calls the abort phrase. Go to [[#The setup is exposed]]. |

### If the crew escapes with the book

**DM NOW:** Nella carries it only if she actually received it. Whoever has it leaves toward Hollis’s freight desk at the active yard. The crew is not heading to an undefined secret hideout.

- **Still in sight:** use ordinary movement, Dash, obstacles, line of sight, and initiative as needed. Nella can Dash as a bonus action; allies cannot. Do not declare escape after one arbitrary failed check.
- **Out of sight:** Hollis’s correspondence on the sorting-floor worktable identifies his freight desk without a roll when searched. A departing handcart also leaves visible tracks from the ramp. The party can follow the lead without another contact chain.
- **At the freight desk:** the surviving crew gathers around the book; use remaining HP and resources. Hollis prefers a deal to public violence. The party may bargain, steal it, or confront them. Real yard workers are witnesses, not extra enemies. Actual Watch intervention ends the staged exercise via [[#The setup is exposed]].
- **Party chooses to stop:** allow an honest return to Edda/Savone. An incomplete recovery need not become an endless pursuit.

## Combat desk

**Use whenever violence begins, including before Merrit is reached.** Keep the current positions; do not reset to starting formation. No reinforcement wave.

1. Roll initiative using the modifiers below; determine surprise from actual awareness.
2. Mark ledger holder, exits, cover, and all participants. Put Merrit on initiative if threatened; he escapes or takes a defensive action.
3. Crew priorities: Hollis pressures the book’s carrier; Rell/Doss obstruct approaches; Nella scouts, evades, and runs only if given the book. They use melee attacks, choose knockout at 0 HP, and do not attack downed targets.
4. Every round, check morale: **Hollis offers a truce at 32 HP or less, or when two crew are out of the fight.** Isolated badly hurt crew may surrender sooner. A visible party surrender stops attacks.
5. Death risk, Watch arrival, or discovery → [[#The setup is exposed]]. Surrender still uses normal timing; it does not negate an attack already resolved.

## 6. Edda and Savone

**DM NOW:** Edda is the new owner, at the South Road office named in the opening papers. No clerk or broker encounter stands between the party and her. She knows only what she has been told; she did not watch the depot. Voice: businesslike, direct.

**EDDA**

> “Is Merrit alive? And what did you have to promise to get this back?”

She verifies the ledger’s stamp and condition, listens to their account, and signs a receipt. If asked to prove identity, she supplies her matching authorization. Inspecting the ledger for identification was allowed. Extra debts were not authorized merely because the party promised them.

**If Merrit accompanies them:** she accepts the book and hears the wage agreement. End the immediate dispute without forcing another negotiation scene. Record any unresolved wage promise for later; it is not a new delivery objective.

**If they have no book:** she asks where it is and what happened. They may make another attempt or report back. Do not create an unexpected forfeiture clause.

**Return to Savone — SAY:**

> “How did it go?”

Let the party speak first. Pay the 150 gp fee for completed delivery; recover unspent advance and record agreed expenses. For incomplete delivery, discuss the actual work and agreement rather than treating truthfulness as completion. Savone cannot quote private incidents before the actors report them.

**Assess what was observed, not a hidden score:**

| Look for | Evidence / consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Competence and coordination | Evidence followed, workable plan, adaptation, recovery, escape. Successful stealth or avoided combat counts. |
| Discretion | What names and contents they disclosed, and to whom. Refusing unnecessary questions is valid. |
| Judgment | Proportionate force, accepted surrender, decisions about Merrit in light of actual risk. Self-defense is expected. |
| Honesty | Truth about custody, damage, money, promises, and failure. Reports arrive separately; unseen acts remain unknown. |

**Trust earned:** Savone arranges a later private conversation about what they want from the Gentleman. He does not reveal his network or an invented new secret now. **Mixed result:** limited access or a future job addressing a particular concern. **Unsafe behavior:** withhold sensitive access; no automatic attack or end to his friendship with Po.

**End:** record actual outcomes in session notes, including injuries, ledger custody, rewards, promises, and whether the setup was exposed. The mission document itself is preparation, not proof these events happened.

## The setup is exposed

**Use immediately if magic, investigation, recognition, serious danger, or the real Watch breaks the arrangement.** Do not make the party finish the route.

**Possible evidence, only when encountered:** uniformly fresh ledger ink; matching emergency hand signals from apparent enemies; a crew member prioritizing a fallen opponent over the book. Insight can suggest a performance; it does not automatically reveal every secret. Spells work normally.

**OPERATIVE — when aborting**

> “The account is closed. Stop. Savone arranged this. We’re putting our weapons down.”

Those who hear disengage, surrender, and offer help. Anyone who did not hear must be informed normally. Aid requires reaching the injured and using actual actions/resources; it is not automatic healing. No new healer or rescue spell appears from nowhere. Incidental witnesses and guards remain independent people.

**SAVONE — when confronted**

> “Yes. I arranged it. Faris trusts you, Po. I needed to know who I would be trusting with you.”
>
> “The names in that book are invented. The next people who might depend on you will not be. That was my reason. It doesn’t oblige you to like it.”

He owns the deception and the risk. The party may refuse further work or resent him. Recognizing the test is evidence of ability, not grounds to fail them. Settle payment and any injury consequences from what actually happened; do not insist that a hurt party should be grateful.
