**What is metagaming?**

When a player uses out-of-character, real-world knowledge to influence their character's decisions, despite that character not knowing that information. This can be knowledge about monsters or even non-combat knowledge.

**Sometimes, metagaming is inevitable.**

If I told you that next session you're fighting a vampire, despite never seeing a vampire stat block, you would probably think to buy wooden stakes, holy water, look for spells that make sunlight, etc because of how much vampire lore there is in.

Sometimes it's just logical. If I put an creature made of ice in front of you, you might logically assume that fire might do more damage and ice might not do a lot of damage. That's just logic.

Sometimes it's easy to forget who knows what as time passes. If Aelar and Aulberos have a heartfelt conversation during their camp watch about the tragic death of Aelar's best friend Steve, Nina and Donna have heard this but technically their characters don't. A year later, it can be hard to remember which character heard that unless Jeremy specifically calls it out.

**What should you do as players?**

Ask! "Would my character know anything about ..."?

We might say yes. We might have you roll. We might say no. 

The best part is that if we say no, you still have ways to learn more about the monsters. The very last thing in the homebrew rules is a system where you, as a bonus action, can roll a knowledge check (arcana, history, nature, or religion depending on the monster type). Depending on how high you roll, you can learn information about the creature.
