A quiet horror known to almost no one — not a teaching of any temple, not a chapter of any bestiary. Only the rare scholar of [[The Cycle]] suspects it, and the rarer creature it has touched never explains itself.

**How one is made:**
- During the height of [[The Umbral Soul]], the dead do not sleep. Spirits and remnants of forgotten ages drift freely, "drawn to what life remains," sustained by hunger, emotion, or memory.
- [[Verda]] is currently held unnaturally at 75% [[The Effulgent Soul]] / 25% [[The Umbral Soul]] (see [[The Cycle]]). That drift never fully stops in this age. It only thins.
- Of every wound a departing soul can carry, one calls to a certain malign spirit louder than the rest: **to be killed by the hand of someone you were infatuated with.** The devotion runs one way for certain — the slain toward the slayer — and whether it was ever returned is the part no one, not even the thing that rises, can say. *(In its most devastating form the love was mutual and equal, ended in the same breath that proved it. But the wound needs only the victim's infatuation to open.)*
- That paradox — to have adored the very hand that ended you — tears something in the soul that ordinary grief, ordinary murder, even ordinary heartbreak does not. Into that tear a **particular evil spirit** pours itself, wearing the body the way water settles into a wound. It is not the wandering remnant of just anyone; it is a specific, patient malice that hunts for exactly this kind of death and waits for it.
- What rises after is not the person who died, and isn't quite a stranger either. It remembers the love. It remembers being killed by it. Both feel equally true, because they are the same truth.
- No scholar has named it properly. Too few have ever been seen and survived comparing notes.

**The two-edged bond:**
- Its hunger always points back to the one it was infatuated with and was killed by. **Feeding on them — and only them — is the one thing that ever quiets it,** even briefly.
- The same bond is its undoing. **It is uniquely vulnerable to that one person.** Their hand can wound and fell it where no stranger's weapon reliably can — which is why [[Corwin Sallow]] could put a crossbow bolt through [[Mella]]'s heart and drop her, twice, while every folk-remedy for her kind (running water, a stake, a refused invitation) does nothing. The leash and the blade are the same tether.
- But note the cruelty: to be *felled* by the beloved's hand is not to be *destroyed.* Short of true unmaking — sunlight, fire, or ruin worked upon its resting place — it only goes still, and returns. The one person who can hurt it most is the one person least able to be rid of it.

**A dire omen:**
- These are **vanishingly rare**, and their appearance is a **bad sign.** One does not arise wherever a spurned lover kills a beloved — the wound alone is not enough. **A Loveslain can only form where the undead presence is already extreme,** the Umbral drift locally thick enough that the particular spirit is present, and waiting, when the death happens.
- So the existence of one is diagnostic. It means that somewhere near — the place of the killing, or the road the body has since traveled — the veil is far thinner than the Gilded city believes possible in an age held at 75% [[The Effulgent Soul]]. A Loveslain in or near [[Thalyra]] is not merely a monster loose in the streets. It is *evidence.*

**DM note:** This is the truth nobody in [[Thalyra]] — least of all [[Corwin Sallow]], and **not even the [[Hall of Arcane Acuity]]** — will ever piece together. The Hall can contain [[Mella]], test her, and confirm she matches nothing in their bestiary, but the *why* is beyond them (see [[Delivering the Devil]]). Per [[Mella]]'s file, do not resolve — even privately — whose hand truly made her. The party never needs any of this to run the encounter; it exists for the DM, and as quiet foreshadowing if the larger Cycle plot ever surfaces: proof the Umbral Soul is bleeding through earlier, more intimately, and in more concentrated pockets than anyone in the Gilded city wants to believe.
